# **COURSE DESCRIPTION** 

This course is designed to help students bridge the gap between theoretical financial models and the world of empirics and financial econometrics. The 10-week class will take students through the major accomplishments (and failures) of empirical finance and financial econometrics during the last quarter of a century. Students will have to read some of the truly seminal works in financial econometrics. Empirical exercises will require students to write their own procedures and to use real market data. These exercises will help us all truly internalize the content of the course. Upon completion of this course, students should: 

- Have a good picture of the main financial econometrics techniques 

- Have a good understanding of the important topics in empirical finance 

- Be able to implement many of the widely used methods in modern empirical finance 

- Have an in-depth knowledge in an area of their choice. 

# **Prerequisites:** 

- A quantitatively based “Investments” class is a prerequisite for the course. 

- Familiarity with computers. Students should be able to download and manipulate data. 

- Knowledge of a programming language or statistical package (e.g., Python, Matlab, R) is required. Knowledge of “canned” statistical programs such as Eviews, SAS, or Stata is not enough. Students will be expected to build their own library of routines and procedures from the ground up.
