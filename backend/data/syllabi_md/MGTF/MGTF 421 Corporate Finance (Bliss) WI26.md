## **DESCRIPTION** 

This course covers the foundations of corporate finance with an emphasis on applications. We will discuss many of the major financial decisions made by managers both within the firm and in their interactions with investors. Topics include criteria for making investment decisions, valuation of financial assets and liabilities, relationships between risk and return and their consequences for the firm, capital structure choice and security issuance, and the effective use of derivatives pricing methods and concepts in corporate finance contexts. 

## **OBJECTIVES** 

The goal of this course is to provide a conceptual framework for understanding and tackling the most important corporate finance problems, and how to apply these concepts in practice. Specifically, the course is meant to: (1) to familiarize students with core corporate finance concepts, (2) to develop a toolkit that allows one to solve important corporate finance problems in practice (including, but not limited to, capital budgeting, valuation, optimal capital structure and security issuance, and uses of derivatives securities and concepts within the firm), and (3) to provide preparation for a variety of roles in corporate finance and capital markets in which knowledge of valuation and financing policy are important.

## **ASSIGNMENTS** 

### **Case assignments (Group work)** 

Written case analyses, which respond to specific questions or prompts in the posted case assignments, are to be prepared by **groups of 3-6 students** (groups with fewer students than this are allowed but are not ideal; groups with more students are _not_ allowed). Each group will submit one write-up as a pdf document, using a drop box on the Canvas course site. The analyses are due at the beginning of class on the day that the case will be discussed. 

**Your case write-ups should reflect the independent judgement and analysis of your group. Making use of case solutions or case materials of any kind from the Internet, from other universities, from previous versions of this course, or from any other sources is strictly prohibited.** 

**The use of generative AI / large language model (LLM) tools such as ChatGPT, Google Gemini, Bing Chat, etc. is** **<u>allowed when preparing your case write-ups, subject to the following conditions:</u>** 

- **You should not use AI tools to generate content that you simply put your name on.** This constitutes an academic integrity violation, just like plagiarizing another student’s work, copying from pre-existing case solutions, or using unauthorized materials during an exam. These tools should support your own work, not replace it! 

- **If you use an AI tool, be transparent about how it was used** by including a relevant footnote or citation, just like if you were citing an academic paper or textbook that you used as a reference. 

- **You are still fully responsible for the content in all deliverables.** If you submit a poor-quality write-up (e.g., one with AI “hallucinations”, logical inconsistencies, responses that are not well-supported by your analysis, etc.) you will receive a correspondingly poor score.
