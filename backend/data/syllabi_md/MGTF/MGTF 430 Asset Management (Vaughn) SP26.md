## **DESCRIPTION** 

This course will provide a rigorous exploration of the use of quantitative tools in asset management, bridging theory with real world investment practice in the quest for generating superior risk-adjusted returns. The primary focus will be equities. The course will have a considerable programming requirement and will utilize Python. 

## **OBJECTIVES** 

At the close of this course, you will be able to: 

- Utilize a comprehensive framework for quantitative investment research. 

- Exhibit practical skills in equity investment signal generation. 

- Implement quantitative investment research tasks in Python. 

- Navigate practical issues a portfolio manager faces in implementing a strategy. 

- Rigorously evaluate investment performance of a strategy and put it in context. 

- Present investment ideas and results in a clear, concise, and organized framework.

There will be a midterm held during the 5<sup>th</sup> week. It will be closed-book, closed-notes, and in-person. Computer usage will not be allowed for the exam. 

## **FINAL PROJECT** 

Groups will be determined during the first two weeks of the course. Project subject proposals will be due on the 5th week of the course. Students are expected to propose, test, and evaluate an original quantitatively-based investment signal. Groups will need to submit their project report and python code by the due date, and will present their project to the class the following week.

## **CHAT-GPT AND RELATED TOOLS** 

Students may use large language model (LLM) tools (e.g., ChatGPT, Gemini, Claude, Grok, Perplexity, etc.) to support their learning, homework assignments, and project work. 

However, all submitted work must reflect the student’s own understanding, analysis, and effort. Reliance on LLM-generated content in bulk or without critical engagement is not permitted. Moreover, certain assignments may restrict or prohibit the use of LLMs, which will be clearly noted in the assignment instructions when applicable. 

Students are responsible for verifying the accuracy and appropriateness of any AI-generated material and must be able to explain and justify all aspects of their work. This policy aligns with professional expectations in the workplace, where AI tools typically may be used to assist with tasks, but individual accountability, accuracy, and judgment remain essential.
