### **DESCRIPTION** 

Many employers in diverse industries are disappointed with college graduates’ writing skills; the disappointment can be especially true in finance-related careers where dependence on data and statistics when communicating can mask, rather than illuminate, key messages. While writing style can be subjective, there are basic principles students can learn and use to build expertise when writing about finance-related topics. Professional Communication in Finance focuses on these principles, their practical application and writing as critical inquiry and creative force. Beyond writing, this class also provides guidance for effective communication when speaking. 

While AI tools such as ChatGPT have proven beneficial to assist with creating slides, written reports and helping with presenting, they should not be a substitute for learning, problem assessment and human judgment. In this course, students may use AI tools, but must attribute where and how they were used. In addition, students may be asked to explain the key assumptions AI may have used in its creative process. The goal? Sharpen students’ critical thinking and help better prepare them for working with AI—not using AI as a replacement for one of the greatest attributes students will bring to the workforce: their ideas; their goals; and their unique voices. 

Clear writing and speaking reflect clear thinking; clear writing and speaking deliver ideas and incite action. Writing talent flows not from inspiration, but from disciplined application, nurtured with time and guidance. Anyone with a basic grasp of English can enhance her/his writing ability. Students will work collaboratively and independently to sharpen their talents, focusing on how data and text can complement and strengthen key messages conveyed in written reports. Students also will learn basics of business journalism which will help shape research questions and underscore the importance of simple, straightforward prose. 

This course will help prepare students for a range of communication tasks they may face on the job. In addition, the course will ask students to reflect on their research and writing processes and challenge them to view themselves as writers so they may add to the critical conversation in their chosen fields. Students will learn to question assumptions, guard against bias, explore, explain, analyze, develop, and critique ideas effectively and make appropriate decisions about argument, structure, and rhetorical conventions. The course consists of lectures that build upon required reading, group discussion, and individual and group writing and speaking projects.The course is built on a simple foundation of four verbs designed to enhance communication ability. These verbs are easy to remember with the acronym: RACE. 

1. **R** esearch: good writing reflects solid investigation, data gathering and striving to understand. 

2. **A** nalyze: apply critical questions to data and transform it into information the audience can use. 

3. **C** ommunicate: deliver key points with text and complementary graphics (charts, tables, etc.) 

4. **E** dit: high-quality writing projects aren’t created once; they are re-written, re-created, revised and polished.

### **ASSIGNMENTS** 

<u>Assignment 1: Before the start of the first class, think about these four questions. Write some notes and</u> come to the first class prepared to tell others your thoughts. 

1. What specific elements make communication effective when writing? 

2. What specific traits make someone an exceptional public speaker? 

3. What traits would you want in a person hired to help enhance your writing and public speaking skills? 

4. Are communication skills important for MQF students AFTER graduation? Give at least one reason why these skills ARE important and one reason why they are NOT important. 

**** Also, we will NOT discuss your answers to this next question in class, but write down a few words that describe “your authentic self.” If you had to sum up the essence of your personality, what 1, 2 or 3 words would you use? 

Group Project: 

You must deliver these things for credit on the group project: 

- PowerPoint slides to accompany your presentation (≥ 10 slides and ≤ 20 slides) 

- a written report of 800 to 1200 words with at least two graphics (charts and/or tables) 

- an in-class presentation of at least 10 minutes, but not more than 15 minutes maximum. Each group member must speak for at least 1 minute. 

- each group member must complete an evaluation of every speaker in _another_ group’s presentation (you will be assigned which other group to evaluate) 

<u>Group Project Report:</u> 

Guidelines/Outline: 

1. Executive Summary 

   - Make this very brief (roughly 200 words) 

   - If a reader only had 30-45 seconds to understand the key points from your research, what would you write? 

2. Introduction 

   - Why is this an interesting and challenging, yet feasible empirical problem? 

   - Brief review of existing work in this area (if relevant) 

   - Outline of the remaining parts of your project 

3. Outline of the Empirical Question 

   - Set up the problem: what is the issue and how will you address it? 

   - How have others tackled the problem, if at all? 

4. Data and Methodology 

   - What are your data sources? 

   - Describe your data 

   - Describe how you conduct your analysis 

5. Results 

   - What did you find? What are the key conclusions? 

   - How are your conclusions the same as and/or different vs. other experts (including AI tools such as ChatGPT or Bard)? 

4 



   - How would you handle objections to your conclusion? What would you tell someone if they said, “You’re wrong.” 

6. Evaluation and Conclusion 

   - Briefly re-state key findings. 

   - What are the practical applications? 

   - Are there other areas to research on this topic? 

7. References (do not include with slides, just the written report) 

   - What academic or practitioner reports, articles, websites or books did you review that helped in your research? 

   - List them alphabetically by primary author’s last/family name. 

   - Give enough details so your reader could find the same report: author, title of the book or article, where published, date, link to a website 

Your PowerPoint slides and Group Project written report can follow this same outline. 

Group Project: 

Pick one of these two options: 

1. Value one of the following four companies analyzed in the Damodaran textbook: Uber; Alibaba; Amazon; or Ferrari. Tell your audience why the stock is either fairly valued, undervalued or over valued at its current price. Start with the data provided in the textbook and update it if necessary. To build your narrative, consider using one of the “Types of Business Stories” listed on page 31 of the Damodaran textbook. 

2. Describe how finance/financial engineering might help improve a business, environmental or social issue. Suggested topics: 

- a. reduce carbon emissions 

- b. enhance recycling rates for plastics 

- c. make insurance more accessible/affordable for homeowners in areas prone to fires 

- d. improve access to fresh water in drought-prone areas 

- e. enhance a sense of belonging for an underrepresented group within a community 

- f.  counter high prices for homes in a city or region 

- g. bring a greater sense of food security/less hunger to a city or country 

- h. strengthen security to battle fraud and other cyber-related crimes 

Your written report should contain a brief description of the problem, the scope/cost of its impact and how a finance-related solution could effect meaningful change. You may wish to address cost savings, positive impact on lives, including specific, quantifiable benefits, feasibility and current obstacles. 

Regardless of the option, properly cite at least 2 research resources (in addition to the textbook if you choose option 1) specific to the finance industry to support your conclusions. 

Also, include at least 2 graphics (charts and/or tables) to support your argument. The total word count should be at least 800 words, but no more than 1,200 words. 

Generally, I will assume the factors/details you cite in the report are accurate. Still, I will spot check and verify some statistics and/or sources you cite. I am most interested in how you write about these elements and how you choose to write and talk about them in your report and presentation.

<u>Written Communication Grading Rubric</u> 

See the following Grading Rubric for guidance on how written assignments will be assessed. 

A. Command of Topic 

- 5 addresses the topic clearly and responds effectively to all aspects of the task 

- 4 clearly addresses the topic, but may respond to some aspects of the task more effectively than others 

- 3 addresses the topic, but may overlook or omit some aspects of the task 

- 2 indicates confusion about the topic or neglects important aspects of the task 

- 1 suggests an inability to comprehend the question or to respond meaningfully to the topic or task 

### B. Communication of Ideas 

- 5 explores the issues thoughtfully and in depth, including relevant financial data; makes clear points 

- 4 shows some depth and complexity of thought and general understanding of data 

- 3 may treat the topic simplistically or repetitively or lack command of data; key point missing or muddled 

- 2 lacks focus or fails to communicate ideas, both in text and graphic formats 

- 1 is unfocused, illogical, incoherent, or disorganized 

C. Organization 

- 5 is coherently organized, with ideas supported by data and well-chosen examples 

- 4 is well organized and developed with appropriate reasons, data and examples 

- 3 is adequately organized and developed, generally supporting ideas with reasons, data and examples 

- 2 is poorly organized and/or undeveloped and/or data does not support key  ideas 

- 1 is undeveloped; provides little or no relevant support 

### D. Syntactic and Data Variety 

- 5 has an effective style marked by syntactic variety, ample data use and command of language 

- 4 displays some syntactic variety in language and solid data use 

- 3 demonstrates adequate facility with syntax and language and data use

- 1 fails to display sentence control and uses a very limited vocabulary along with poor use of supporting data

- 3 Generally demonstrates control of mechanics, usage, sentence structure and data use
