## DESCRIPTION 

This course introduces students to cutting-edge data sources and AI methodologies in financial applications. Beginning with an overview of alternative data, such as price charts, students will explore techniques for analyzing these data and will be exposed to the latest methodologies in the field. The course emphasizes how such data and methods can improve financial decision-making, particularly in shaping portfolio choices, systematic strategies, and trading signals. 

## OBJECTIVES 

The course aims to provide students with an overview of the latest advances in alternative data sources and the AI tools used for analyzing financial markets and making financial decisions. Additionally, it equips students with a toolkit to analyze these datasets and stay up to date with the latest research as it becomes available. The ultimate goal of the course is to give students insights on how alternative data and AI tools can be used by industry players — such as asset managers, investors, trading firms — to improve their decision-making process. As such, the course will emphasize practical financial applications through hands-on programming and data analysis. Guest speakers from industry will share practical perspectives on how research developments are being applied in real-world financial settings. Finally, students will have the opportunity to apply the techniques covered during the course of their group project.

## STRUCTURE AND TOPICS 

We begin by reviewing new data sources and learning how they are mapped into the statistical domain. Next, we learn to apply the latest econometric and machine learning tools to extract statistical information and patterns that serve as key inputs for financial decision-making. The emphasis is on the underlying financial problems and how these are shaped by the latest advancements in AI. Finally, we investigate issues that may arise from an incorrect use of the methodologies we learn during the course. 

## SCHEDULE 

1. Introduction to alternative and unstructured data sources; 

2. Structure and modeling of alternative data; 

3. AI methodologies to analyze alternative data; 

4. Review of the latest research in finance; 

5. Financial applications. 

## GRADING AND EVALUATION 

- Group Project (70%) : students are required to design, execute, and craft a research idea which is rooted around the topics of the course. The project must be done in group, and the purpose is to mimic how quant research pods work in quantitative investment firms. 

- Project Presentation (20%): students will present (pitch) their research idea to the classroom. The object should be to convince the audience of the quality of their idea. 

- Class Participation (10%): actively engage and following classes.
