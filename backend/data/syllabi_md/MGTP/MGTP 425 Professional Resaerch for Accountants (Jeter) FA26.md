## **COURSE DESCRIPTION** 

This accelerated 2-unit course meets California State Board of Accountancy requirements for focused instruction in accounting and tax research. Students will learn to locate, interpret, and apply authoritative guidance from sources such as the FASB Codification, Internal Revenue Code, PCAOB/AICPA standards, and SEC releases, using professional research platforms including Thomas Reuters Checkpoint Edge and CCH IntelliConnect. The course emphasizes the IRAC (Issue, Rule, Application, Conclusion) framework, professional memo writing, and case-based exercises. Students will also explore the emerging role of artificial intelligence and data analytics in research, preparing them for the evolving demands of the accounting profession. 

## **OBJECTIVES** 

By the close of this course, you will be able to: 

- Identify reseaIdentify and clearly articulate researchable accounting, auditing, and tax issues. 

- Distinguish between authoritative and nonauthoritative research sources. 

- Navigate and search the FASB Accounting Standards Codification efficiently. 

- Locate and apply relevant PCAOB and AICPA auditing standards. 

- Conduct tax research using primary and secondary tax authorities and professional research databases. 

- Apply a systematic professional research process to unfamiliar fact patterns. 

- Analyze authoritative guidance rather than merely locating relevant citations. 

- Draft concise professional research memoranda that clearly present issues, authority, analysis, and conclusions. 

- Evaluate appropriate and inappropriate uses of artificial intelligence in professional accounting research.

## **ARTIFICIAL INTELLIGENCY (AI) POLICY** 

This course will actively explore the role of artificial intelligence (AI) tools, including large language models (LLMs), in accounting research. Students are encouraged to experiment with AI methods as supplemental aids to enhance efficiency and explore emerging trends. However, authoritative sources— such as the FASB Codification, PCAOB and AICPA standards, Internal Revenue Code, and SEC releases—must always serve as the foundation for research assignments, memos, and case analyses. 

Permitted Use: Students may use AI tools to the extent discussed in course readings and lectures, with a focus on understanding how AI can complement but not replace authoritative research. 

Disclosure Requirement: Any use of AI in assignments must be clearly disclosed in a short note (e.g., “AI was used to generate an outline for this memo; authoritative citations and analysis are my own”). 

Limitations: AI tools are known to produce errors or fabrications. Students are responsible for verifying all information against authoritative sources. 

Academic Integrity: Failure to disclose AI use, or submitting AI-generated work without proper verification and citation of authoritative sources, may be considered a violation of academic integrity standards. 

This policy is consistent with UC San Diego’s guidance: AI may be used for support, but students are expected to complete their own work, demonstrate independent analysis, and ensure the accuracy of all submissions.
