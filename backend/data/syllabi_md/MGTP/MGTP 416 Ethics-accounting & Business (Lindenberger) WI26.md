# **DESCRIPTION** 

Being an accountant carries enormous responsibility. You have a duty to your clients, your colleagues and the public to act with integrity and honesty. This course will provide you with a strong basis for identifying, evaluating and responding to ethical issues you will encounter as an accounting and business professional. This includes review of various professional codes of conduct (AICPA, SEC, TSBPA and IMA). 

# **OBJECTIVES** 

At the close of this course, you will be able to: 

- Understand the key concepts underpinning ethical theory 

- Describe important considerations in applying ethical reasoning to potential ethical dilemmas 

- Describe and comply with professional codes of conduct, including the AICPA, SEC, TSBPA and IMA ethics rules 

- Define your professional responsibilities in the context of professional ethics, including understanding the concepts of integrity, objectivity, independence and other core values of the accounting profession, and applying these concepts to potential ethical dilemmas 

- Analyze, apply professional judgment to and make decisions about current and emerging ethical issues facing the accounting profession and clients 

- Understand your role in corporate governance and ethical management

# **APPENDIX A: ACADEMIC INTEGRITY** 

# **<u>General</u>** 

Academic Integrity is expected of everyone at UCSD. This means that you must be honest, fair, responsible, respectful and trustworthy in all your actions. I will not tolerate lying, cheating or any other forms of dishonesty because such actions undermine learning and the University’s ability to certify your knowledge and abilities. Thus, I will report to the Academic Integrity Office any attempt to get, or help another get, a grade by cheating, lying or dishonesty, which will result in sanctions, up to and including an F in this class and suspension or dismissal from the University. So, please think carefully before you act and ask yourself the following questions: 

1. Is my action honest, fair, respectful, responsible and trustworthy? 

2. Is my action authorized by the instructor? 

If you are unsure, don’t ask a friend – ask me, an instructional assistant or the Academic Integrity Office. 

(Source: Tricia Bertram Gallant, Ph.D., UCSD Academic Integrity Office, 2017)

# **<u>Use of Artificial Intelligence (AI) for Coursework</u>** 

The use of AI tools such as ChatGPT, Bing Chat, Bard, Claude, etc. is encouraged in this course. These tools have considerable potential to streamline and augment our work and you should use them for this class with the following caveats and conditions: 

1. These tools should augment your work, not replace it. For instance, large language models can improve the quality of your writing, identify new sources, provide high-level summaries of complex ideas or terms, and pressure test your arguments for logical inconsistencies. These tools can help you come up with new ideas, overcome writer’s block, or refine your original ideas. You should not use Generative AI tools to generate content that you simply put your name on. This constitutes an academic integrity violation, much like taking another individual’s work and misrepresenting it as your own. 

2. I will assume all students have access to and will use Generative AI and LLMs. If your work does not exceed the baseline quality level produced by AI, you should expect a low grade (C-, D). 

3. If you use Generative AI for an assignment, be transparent about if/how it was used in a footnote or citation (e.g., link to the ChatGPT chat you created). 

4. Large language models have documented issues with hallucination and inaccuracy (e.g., generating fake citations, failing logical arguments, misrepresenting sources, etc.). You are fully responsible for the content you submit in your assignments and will be graded accordingly.
