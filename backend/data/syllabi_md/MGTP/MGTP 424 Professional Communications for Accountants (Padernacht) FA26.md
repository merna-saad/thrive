## **DESCRIPTION** 

Oral communication is a critical part of organizational life and your success as an Accounting professional. Effective oral communication is the exchange of information critical to influencing others, working in groups, getting along with colleagues, and managing organizational politics. As an Accounting professional, your prospective employer and colleagues will depend on you to effectively describe and explain thoughts, opinions, perspectives, updates, data and statistics. 

This course is designed to prepare you for job-related communication tasks. You will learn how to think purposefully, creatively, and critically about your one-on-one and group communications, such as when presenting in formal meetings, speaking informally with co-workers, or communicating remotely online. When communicating orally, you will learn to take the recipient(s) of your message into account. While oral communication styles can vary and be subjective, you will learn basic principles to build your capabilities when speaking about Accounting and Finance-related topics. 

The course consists of lectures and in-class activities that build upon required reading, group discussions, individual speaking assignments, and team projects.  The learning process will involve implementing communication concepts, ideas, strategies, and practical approaches between and during class – meaning you will be orally communicating within your teams and during most class sessions, and you will use constructive feedback to learn, improve, and develop. In addition, you will be asked to reflect on your own and others’ communication processes by learning how to question assumptions, guard against preconceptions, explore, explain, analyze, develop, and critique ideas effectively, and make appropriate decisions about argument, structure and rhetorical conventions. 

## **OBJECTIVES** 

- References to the “audience” or “recipient” apply to various Accounting profession-related groups, stakeholders, and cultures. This course aims to provide the skills and knowledge for you to be able to: 1. **Demonstrate** clear, confident, and professional oral communication in formal and informal Accounting-related interactions, reflecting an understanding of different audiences. 

2. **Persuade** audiences orally using sound arguments based on facts and reliable evidence, insightful research queries, and identifying assumptions. 

3. **Apply** effective interpersonal and digital communication strategies in team collaborations and Accounting client interactions. 

4. **Receive** feedback non-defensively and provide constructive feedback with your peers to foster a continuous learning environment. 

5. **Use** observational skills to discern your own and others’ communication styles and nonverbal behavior to adapt interpersonal strategies to different audiences and enhance interactions. 

6. **Identify** and **leverage** your communication strengths and develop areas needing improvement.

## **Guiding Principles** 

By participating in this course, students agree to abide by the following:

2. If you don’t understand something, ask about it; others will likely have similar questions.

There are two types of assignments, primarily based on your team’s project: Individual ( **IND** ) and Team ( **TEAM** ) assignments. While some weeks will require an individual assignment, each week you will meet with your project team to develop a presentation of your research using **APPENDIX B** as your guide. Below are four examples of team project topics from previous class teams. While these are no longer available, they provide useful ideas to reference when developing your own project team’s topic. 

**Example 1** : Value the stock of one of the following four companies analyzed in the recommended reading, Damodaran, A. (2017) _Narrative and Numbers_ : Uber; Alibaba; Amazon; or Ferrari. Explain to your audience why the stock is either fairly valued, undervalued or over valued at its current price. (Use the most recent price available and state the stock price and date in the report and in the presentation.) Start with the data provided and update it to the current year. Include a brief description of the company and its business, an overview of the industry in which the company operates, and key fundamental, technical and/or macroeconomic factors supporting your recommendation. 

**Example 2** : Evaluate the effects of increasing interest in Environmental, Social, and Governance (ESG) principles in the accounting or consulting industry. Provide a definition of ESG and describe how ESG is applied in Accounting. Explain to your audience why interest in ESG is rising and whether you believe ESG principles have merit. If so, why? If not, why not?  Also describe what effects ESG principles are having on the Accounting industry in terms of benefits, cost, effort and time. Consider whether standards are clear and widely accepted/adopted and the potential implications if they are not. Have any firms or individuals emerged as leaders in this field? If so, why? What are the business opportunities ESG principles create? What business opportunities may be eroding due to ESG? Other topics you may wish to analyze: how firms are communicating their commitment to ESG and whether you believe it is effective; key questions and potential answers firms should be asking and answering around ESG principles; what additional/new areas may emerge given ESG’s popularity; whether ESG’s popularity will continue or decline. 

**Example 3** : With publicly traded companies holding an increasing amount of goodwill and intangible assets on their balance sheets, what are the implications for the companies, the industry and investors? Why has this increase occurred and is it likely to continue, level off or decline? Has Accounting kept up with this trend? Why or why not? What does the future hold for Accounting for goodwill and intangible assets? Consider addressing the evolution of goodwill/intangible accounting and whether the issue could have/should have been addressed earlier. If so, how? If not, why not? 

**Example 4** : What are the biggest issues companies face in terms of data security? What are the implications—for the company, for the public, for the industry? How can we best address these issues and what are the benefits? What are related costs in terms of time, effort and/or money? How have global standards for data privacy evolved—and have they evolved quickly enough? What are best practices in this area—how long companies keep data, data destruction, data auditing, etc.? Are they sufficient? What new insights can you bring to a client or prospect with regard to this issue?

## **Policy on the Use of Artificial Intelligence (AI) Communication Tools** 

## **Core Principles and Course Assignments** 

Students are expected to complete their preparation independently before using AI or related tools for enhancement. These tools are intended to support the development of communication skills, not to replace them. Students are fully responsible for all presented content and are expected to be transparent about their use of AI or coaching tools. 

Course assignments are either in-person or video-recorded presentations. Students are evaluated on presence, delivery, structure, and ability to engage and respond in real time. AI tools are preparation resources; they cannot replicate the skills developed through practice and independent thinking. 

## **Approved Uses** 

Students may use approved tools for the following purposes: 

- Organizing arguments and developing a narrative arc, 

- Refining talking points and improving clarity of expression, 

- Simulating audience questions and exploring counterarguments, 

- Reviewing slide structure and presentation flow, and 

- Practicing and improving delivery through dedicated coaching applications. 

As the quarter progresses and assignments become increasingly self-directed, students are expected to develop their own topic, angle, and voice. AI tools may support that process but should not drive it. 

## **Prohibited Uses** 

Students may not use AI or related tools to: 

- Generate a verbatim script or deliver AI-authored content as their own, 

- Select a topic or develop a core argument on their behalf, and 

- Access proprietary course materials.

Effective communication depends on the speaker's credibility, conviction, and presence — qualities that cannot be generated by AI. Students are encouraged to use these tools the way a skilled athlete uses coaching: to arrive better prepared and more confident, while recognizing that performance itself cannot be delegated.
