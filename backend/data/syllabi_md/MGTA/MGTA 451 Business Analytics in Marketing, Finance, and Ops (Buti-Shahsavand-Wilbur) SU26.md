### **3. Assessments and Grading** 

The marketing module component will count for ⅓ of your MGTA 451 grade. The marketing module grade will be determined by class contribution (⅓), day 1 activities (⅓), and day 2 activities (⅓). All grades will be curved and should be interpreted as relative, not absolute.

### **4. Communication** 

We meet in person to facilitate two-way open-ended interactions. You should bring your questions, comments and observations to the class, to test your understanding, and to enable peer-to-peer learning. It is essential that you arrive prepared. We move fast, so there is no second chance to have a productive classroom experience. 

We require Piazza for asynchronous communication, available here. Main reasons: 

- 1) Piazza enables students to answer each other’s questions. Peer answers tend to be fast and accurate, and therefore encourage efficiency and fairness. Answers can inform other students, and help to ensure that everyone can reference the same information. 

- 2) Piazza enables private messages to ‘All Instructors’ for personal situations such as illness. This is the place to notify us in advance if you have to miss class for a university-sanctioned reason. Messages must be addressed to All Instructors so the Professor & IA can coordinate and share records. 

By default, any Piazza message should be visible to classmates, unless it contains personal or confidential information. You can post anonymously to classmates if you prefer, but we encourage use of names as the default. If you are posting confidential or private information, please post it to ‘All Instructors’ so the professor and IA can both access it. 

We discourage use of emails and canvas/instructure messages, as they forego most Piazza benefits. We kindly ask you to note our communication preference, and in return we promise to answer Piazza messages faster than any other asynchronous medium. If you email us, we will ask you to kindly post your message to Piazza before we respond.

### **DESCRIPTION** 

This short course is designed to provide strong foundations in the fundamental concepts of Finance.  We will discuss key concepts, techniques and calculations, as well as applications to real world situations.  The course is structured as a combination of lectures, in-class exercises, discussions, and assignments. 

There are no prerequisites to this course. 

### **OBJECTIVES** 

By the end of the course, students are expected to possess the skills for: 

- _using time value of money concepts_ 

- _applying investment decision rules_ 

- _evaluating and choosing projects_ 

- _understanding the basics of asset valuation_ 

- _effectively comparing risk and return tradeoffs in a wide variety of financial dilemmas_ 

- _recognizing how diversification leads to better portfolio outcomes_
