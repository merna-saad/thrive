## **DESCRIPTION** 

This is a hands-on, project-based course in which you will learn to transform today’s high-volume data from disparate systems into real-time, readily actionable, business insights via highly interactive charts, maps, and graphs. We will be using one of the top BI tools in the industry, Tableau, but the principles of good data visualization and dashboard design will be readily transferable to any modern BI tools.  In addition to developing a very marketable skill, your finished project will make a nice addition to a work product portfolio. 

## **OBJECTIVES** 

At the close of this course, you will be able to: 

- Articulate the value of BI systems 

- Employ the principles of good infographic design to convey information rapidly and accurately 

- • Recognize when design may lead to poor interpretation or even be designed to deceive (a nice life skill) 

- Evaluate related BI technologies 

- Identify data sources and plan BI Implementation 

- Combine, cleanse, and group data feeds for dashboard creation 

- Design and build an effective, interactive dashboard 

- Present effectively, using data story views

## **_Team Projects_** 

Small student teams will work together to design and implement a story of informative dashboards that combine multiple data sources, and teams will use their dashboards to tell a story in their final presentation.  The group project will comprise four components, and there will be one deliverable for each team: 

1. _Dashboard Design Document_ : This is a 2-5 page document with simple sketches of expected elements and the intended interaction between those elements.  The purpose of this document is to thoughtfully consider the business use of the dashboard, the end-user’s expected capability and needs, and then apply the principles of good design, prior to developing any views or dashboards. 

2. _Tableau’s Prep Builder Workflow_ : will integrate two or more datasets, with at least four appropriate cleaning components. 

3. _Dashboard and Presentation_ : At least 3 dashboards with 6 or more views (each student on the team should individually create at least two views) will be used to tell a story about the data analyzed in a classroom presentation of 10-15 minutes, and answer questions from reviewers. (extra credit is available for good use of storyboard) 

4. _Final BI Project Document_ : Less than 5 pages of text, along with any appropriate images, to describe the project goals, team roles, data sources and prep, dashboard interactions and findings.  Additionally, enhancements from the Design Document should be considered relative to business goals, intended audience or useability. 

## **_Peer and Team Presentation Reviews_** 

Each student will complete a short survey of each other team member, relative to quantity of work, value of work, and degree of team co-operation. 

Each student will attend four other team presentations, and provide simple feedback on those teams per a rubric to be provided.
