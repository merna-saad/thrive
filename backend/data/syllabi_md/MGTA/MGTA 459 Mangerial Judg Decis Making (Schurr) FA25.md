### **DESCRIPTION** 

This course is composed of two parts.  They are quite distinct yet critically connected: the first is about deriving better analyses and solutions, and the second is about implementing them. 

In our initial sessions, we will examine how people make judgments and decisions, concentrating on how people perceive and react to uncertainty and variability.  We will lay out key implications for business strategies.  Consider a biotech start-up that is attempting to develop a new cancer drug. Suppose its managers must choose between divergent research tracks and market niches, each of which shows some promise but also carries some risks.  How will they weigh the promise versus the risks, evaluate relevant information, and settle on a way to proceed?  The hope is that a savvy understanding of the answers to such questions can inform your approach to many business settings. 

Of course, it is not enough to be skillful at analysis.  To succeed in business, you must also be adept at engaging others and getting their buy-in and follow through.  For instance, the managers of the biotech start-up may wish to motivate employees and other stakeholders to devote their energies to a particular research approach.  They may wish to rally investors around funding this approach. Accordingly, we will also discuss how to best build, protect, and exert influence to achieve your goals.  A key idea is that developing multiple tools of influence is beneficial because different circumstances require different approaches. 

### **OBJECTIVES** 

At the close of this course, you should better understand 

- How people think about uncertainty and variability 

- How to design effective decision processes 

- What influence tactics you tend to rely on 

- How to construct a plan for expanding the set of influence tactics you use

### **GRADING** 

Your grade will be based on participation (25%), a midterm (35%) and a final project (40%). All assignments are individual. 

_PARTICIPATION (25%)_ 

Classroom discussion is an integral part of this course.  Much of your learning will come from the insights we are able to achieve by collectively mulling course concepts.  Moreover, to fully engage with the course material you need to be actively involved in the discussion. 

Because it is vital to your learning and the learning of your colleagues, your participation is assessed and factored into your grade.  I make notes after each class regarding each student’s role in the discussion.  So does the teaching assistant.  Near the end of the term, I will solicit your input about which students best-facilitated your learning. 

We are all jointly responsible for the quality of the discussion.  I would like for you to focus on being prepared, engaged and respectful of others.  We want to cultivate a direct but friendly environment that encourages honesty and risk-taking.  Mistakes are okay, especially as long as we are striving for insight.  Irreverence is okay.  Questions others know the answer to are okay.  As long as you are prepared, engaged and respectful, almost anything goes.

_MIDTERM EXAM (35%)_ 

We will have a take-home midterm after the first part of the course.  It will be open-book, opennote, individual work.  It will consist of a combination of multiple choice and short answer questions.  Some of the material on the exam will appear both in class and in the readings, but some of the material will appear either only in class or only in the readings. 

FINAL PROJECT – PERSONAL ACTION PLAN (40%) 

Your final project is to put together a personal distillation of concepts from the second part of our course.  This can take any form – paper, powerpoint, website, video, art project, song, game, etc. Literally any form that works for you. You can think of it as one of those “cheat sheets” that some stats/econ profs allow for their exams. The idea is to boil down as much as possible of what you consider to be the most useful information from the course into a format you can carry forward. The goal is for you to use these ideas in the future. This is intended as a vehicle for that.
