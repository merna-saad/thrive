# **DESCRIPTION** 

Businesses and policymakers are increasingly using experimentation in a wide range of areas including product pricing, marketing strategies, employee incentives and shaping consumer behavior. These experiments can be particularly effective if the interventions tested incorporate insights from behavioral economics.  However, many companies currently lack the knowledge necessary to take advantage of experimentation. An understanding of how to design and run a good experiment will position students at the cutting edge of decision-making in business. 

The goals of the course are for students to understand: (1) the role of experiments in business and policy (2) how insights from behavioral economics can be incorporated into experimental interventions and (3) how to design and run an experiment in an organization. 

We will begin by discussing the role of experiments in organizations and examining examples. Students will work individually or in teams to develop a question and design a randomized experiment to test their question. Students will run pilot studies to inform their designs. At the end of the course, students will present their pilots. 

# **OBJECTIVES** 

At the close of this course, you will be able to: 

- Understand the role of experimentation in business and policy 

- Incorporate insights from behavioral economics into experimental interventions 

- Design and implement randomized experiments 

- Develop experiments that address the interests of businesses and policymakers

# **AI POLICY** 

While generative AI can be a very useful tool, in this class we will not be using AI for assignments or exams (unless your pilot study involves the impact of AI in the pilot study). We would like you to practice coming up with your own ideas and experimental designs because we believe that this process teaches critical thinking skills that are essential in business.
