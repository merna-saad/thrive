## **DESCRIPTION** 

This course provides a rigorous and practical introduction to healthcare decision modeling and analytics. We will start with cost-effectiveness analysis (CEA), which is the cornerstone of evidence-based resource allocation in healthcare. Students will learn how to evaluate competing health interventions by quantifying their costs and health outcomes using formal decision analysis methods. Building on this foundation, students will learn how to address uncertainty in decision models through deterministic and probabilistic sensitivity analyses. We will then focus on dynamic modeling frameworks widely used in health economics and policy analysis. Students will develop Markov cohort models and microsimulation (patientlevel) models to represent chronic disease progression, competing risks, and patient heterogeneity, and analyze model outputs and visualize results. Through a combination of lectures, hands-on exercises, and real-world healthcare examples, students will gain the analytical and computational skills to build, analyze, and interpret decision models that inform health policy decisions. 

## **PREREQUISITES** 

MGTA 453 or knowledge of probability and statistics. 

## **OBJECTIVES** 

Upon completion of this course, students will be able to: 

- Understand the fundamental principles of healthcare decision modeling and analytics. 

- • Construct and analyze decision models to evaluate the cost-effectiveness of healthcare interventions under uncertainty. 

- Develop analytic solutions to practical health decision making problems for transforming data into knowledge. 

- Apply deterministic and probabilistic sensitivity analysis techniques to assess the robustness of model outcomes. 

- Develop and implement Markov cohort and microsimulation models to simulate disease progression, treatment effects, and patient heterogeneity. 

- Integrate utility measurement methods into decision models and critically evaluate how outcome valuation choices influence cost-effectiveness results and policy recommendations.
