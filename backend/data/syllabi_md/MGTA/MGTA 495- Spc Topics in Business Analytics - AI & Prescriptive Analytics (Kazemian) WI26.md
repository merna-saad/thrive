## **DESCRIPTION** 

This course provides an integrated introduction to Artificial Intelligence (AI), optimization, and simulation techniques that form the foundation of prescriptive analytics. In the first half of the course, students explore core AI and deep learning methods that enable data-driven prediction, pattern recognition, computer vision, natural language processing (NLP), and large language models (LLMs). We will also discuss model interpretability, fairness, and the mitigation of algorithmic bias in AI models. The second half of the course transitions to prescriptive analytics and discusses how simulation and optimization methods can guide data-driven analytics and decision-making in business. Topics include mixed-integer programming (MIP) for resource allocation and planning, dynamic programming for sequential decisionmaking under uncertainty, and discrete-event simulation for modeling complex systems in operations, finance, and healthcare. Through a combination of lectures, real-world examples, and hands-on coding labs, students will gain a practical and conceptual understanding of how modern AI, simulation, and mathematical modeling techniques can be applied to optimize processes, improve outcomes, and support strategic decision-making in various business domains. 

## **OBJECTIVES** 

Upon completion of this course, students will be able to: 

- Understand foundational concepts of artificial intelligence (AI), mathematical optimization, and simulation modeling, and how they can help guide business decisions. 

- Apply deep learning methods, including multilayer perceptrons (MLPs), convolutional neural networks (CNNs), and natural language processing models, to solve prediction and pattern recognition problems across various business domains. 

- Evaluate the interpretability, fairness, and ethical implications of AI models, identifying potential sources of bias and employing strategies to improve transparency and accountability in AI-driven decision support systems. 

- Build and analyze discrete-event simulation (DES) models to assess system performance under uncertainty and compare alternative strategies. 

- Formulate and solve prescriptive analytics problems using mathematical optimization techniques, including linear, integer, and dynamic programming, to support data-driven decision-making. 

- Critically assess analytical models and results, understanding the underlying assumptions, strengths, and limitations of AI, optimization, and simulation methods when applied to complex, real-world decision problems.

## **COURSE POLICIES** 

**<u>Course Format.</u>** <u>The course will be offered in person and will consist of 10 weekly sessions. Sessions will</u> include interactive lectures and hands-on coding exercises in Python. Students are encouraged to bring their laptops to follow along with lecture notes and coding exercises. All lectures will be recorded. 

**<u>Attendance Policy</u>** <u>. In-person attendance is mandatory for all students. If you must miss a class due to a</u> valid reason (e.g., illness or family emergency), you must email the instructor with appropriate documentation (proof of valid reason) and watch the lecture recording in full within 7 days of the missed class to receive the attendance credit. Missing more than 3 classes will result in a failing grade (“F”) for the course – no exceptions. 

**<u>Expectations and Late Submissions</u>** . Students will be expected to complete five homework assignments, participate in class discussions, and take a midterm exam (take-home) and a final exam. All assignments must be turned in via Canvas by the specified deadline. Late submission will incur a penalty of 10% of the total points for each 24-hour period (or part thereof) past the deadline, and submissions are not accepted after solutions are posted on Canvas (usually 2-3 days after the due date). Students are encouraged to submit their assignments early if they anticipate delays closer to the due date. 

**<u>Use of AI Tools such as ChatGPT.</u>** Generative AI (e.g., ChatGPT) can be used to brainstorm ideas for your assignments and assist in debugging your code. However, you should understand and explain every step of your analysis in your submission. You should also fully understand and be able to explain all details of every line of code that you submit as your own work. Any other use of GenAI requires preapproval and must be documented and submitted along with the relevant assignment. Failure to submit documentation or identify use of AI is considered an integrity violation: <u>https://academicintegrity.ucsd.edu/excel-integrity/gen-ai/index.html</u> 

**<u>Changes.</u>** The instructors reserve the right to make changes during the term to any aspect of the syllabus that, in their judgment, are needed to achieve the learning objectives of the course.
