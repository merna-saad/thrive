## **DESCRIPTION** 

Data is one of the most valuable assets in modern organizations, but its value depends on the ability to efficiently access, transform, and analyze it. This course provides students with the foundational and practical skills needed to work with data in real-world business environments through Structured Query Language (SQL) and Extract, Transform, and Load (ETL) processes. SQL remains the industry standard for interacting with relational databases and is one of the most sought-after technical skills in analytics, consulting, finance, marketing, operations, technology, and data science roles. 

Students will learn how organizations store, manage, and retrieve data from enterprise databases, and how to write effective SQL queries to answer business questions, generate insights, and support datadriven decision-making. Beyond querying data, the course introduces ETL concepts and workflows that enable analysts to collect data from multiple sources, clean and transform it, and prepare it for reporting, visualization, and advanced analytics. These skills form the backbone of modern business intelligence and analytics systems. 

As organizations increasingly rely on large-scale data infrastructure, business analytics professionals are expected not only to interpret results but also to understand where data originates, how it is structured, and how it moves through analytical pipelines. Mastery of SQL and ETL empowers students to work more independently, communicate effectively with data engineers and technical teams, ensure data quality, and unlock insights that might otherwise remain inaccessible. 

By the end of the course, students will be able to design and execute SQL queries of increasing complexity, integrate and transform data from diverse sources, and build reproducible data workflows that support business analysis. These capabilities are essential for success in graduate-level analytics coursework and are highly valued by employers seeking professionals who can bridge the gap between business decision-making and modern data infrastructure. 

## **OBJECTIVES** 

- Understand and apply foundational database concepts to organize, manage, and access business data effectively. 

- Retrieve and manipulate data using SQL to answer business questions, generate insights, and support data-driven decision-making. 

- Apply advanced SQL techniques, including joins, subqueries, aggregations, and common table expressions, to analyze complex datasets and solve analytical problems. 

- Design and implement ETL workflows that extract, transform, and load data from multiple sources into structured, analysis-ready datasets.

## **CLASS POLICY ON THE USE OF ARTIFICIAL INTELLIGENCE TOOLS** 

The use of artificial intelligence (AI) tools such as ChatGPT, Bing Chat, Bard, Claude, etc. is encouraged in this class. These tools have considerable potential to streamline and augment our work. You should use them with the following caveats and conditions: 

- AI-tools should enhance your work, not replace it. For instance, large language models (LLMs) can improve the quality of your writing, identify new sources, provide high-level summaries of complex ideas or terms, generate code, and more. These tools can help you come up with new ideas, overcome writer’s block, and refine your original ideas. You should not use Generative AI tools to generate content that you simply put your name on. This constitutes an academic integrity violation, much like taking another individual’s work and misrepresenting it as your own. 

- You may utilize Generative AI and LLMs for class preparation and for help with assignments. However, we require that you fully understand and can explain, in-person or on video, all details of every line of code and mathematical calculation that you submit as your own work. 

- If you use AI for an assignment, be transparent about how it was used in a footnote or citation (e.g., a link to the ChatGPT chat that you created). 

- Be aware that LLMs have documented issues with hallucination and inaccuracy (e.g., generating fake python packages, failing logical arguments, and misrepresenting sources). You are fully responsible for all content you submit in your assignments and will be graded accordingly.
