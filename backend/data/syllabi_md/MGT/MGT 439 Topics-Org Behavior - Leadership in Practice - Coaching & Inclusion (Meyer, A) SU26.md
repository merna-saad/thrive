The people working in a company are the biggest asset to the success of the business. But people are complicated. Every employee is different and wants to be seen for their unique value. This complexity can be a challenge. What practices help you develop the conditions where people do their best work? 

## COURSE OBJECTIVES 

Through the two tools of coaching and inclusion, students build their leadership mindset and hands-on skillset. We examine people-management practices to explore how, as a leader of people, do you: 

1. Manage people in order to engage and get the best out of each employee, regardless of their style or level? 

2. Grow your employees and yourself, so that you scale your impact. 

3. Create inclusive environments where people feel they belong and are comfortable with creative friction so they leverage their work to deploy collective genius?

The course sets the stage with a foundational examination of the roles of the manager and the leader. Then moving into skills development, we start with focus on the individual employee through the tool of coaching. Students first learn the basic process of coaching others and receive coaching. Then through deliberate practice, gradually students increase their comfort and agility into ambiguous coaching situations to problem-solve. 

Next, looking at groups of people, we explore the paradox of diversity--that diversity among groups of people leads to higher innovation yet also leads to conflict and exclusion. Students work with both behavior and process to develop learning environments where everybody thrives. Outcomes such as whether employees feel they belong, how the culture “feels” to them, and how to balance creative friction can be a result of process and structure, as well as behavior. 

This course also guides people managers to look at leadership through the lens of how they think about and interact with their people. This examination culminates in a forward-looking leadership vision, values and practices statement. 

## INSTRUCTIONAL METHODOLOGY 

This class is highly experiential and requires your mental and physical focus. Come to class as well-rested, fed and prepared as is possible (for a person going to graduate school and working full time). 

We will work in three modes: preparation, performance, and reflection. This model comes from the theater, but is essential in sports, business and any kind of human performance. In more detail: 

1. Prepare: Preparation involves practice, routines, setting intention, planning, rehearsal, and experimentation. Part of performance preparation is determining how you need to prepare. 

2. Perform: Performance involves letting go of the preparation, to be present with what is happening in the moment in order to respond authentically. 

Regarding the act of performance, a tennis player cycles serve-by-serve, set-by-set, game-by-game, starting over and over again. The actor gets on stage night after night and starts from scratch with a new audience. Push yourself to make mistakes in performance so you learn. 

3. Reflect: Reflection involves looking back at what happened, exploring what was meaningful about it and assessing what you have learned/will do differently, so that you can integrate that learning “next time.” 

“The debrief is as important as the game.” – Steve Ballmer
