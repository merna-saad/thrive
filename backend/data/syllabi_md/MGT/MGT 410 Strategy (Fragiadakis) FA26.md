## **DESCRIPTION** 

This course focuses on strategic decision making by companies in competitive markets. The approach will combine applied economic theory with empirical examples to develop competitive advantage and game theory frameworks. Classes will consist of both lecture and discussion of applications, including case studies. 

## **OBJECTIVES** 

At the close of this course, you will be able to: 

- Identify sources of profit within and across industries 

- Understand how to create and sustain a business’s competitive advantage 

- Direct how a company should grow, innovate and respond to changing markets 

- Analyze strategic interactions between competing and complementary companies 

- Shape the nature of industry competition to your advantage 

- Use strategic frameworks to communicate within and across  businesses

## _<u>Goals</u>_ 

The goal of these assignments is to have you practice the skills necessary to do competitive analysis on your own. Given that, the structure of the assignment is designed to match the structure of a competitive analysis you will do on the job. 

Executives making strategic decisions often have limited time. Thus we will require these deliverables to be very short but very substantive. Each portion of the assignment will require significant thinking and refining. The teams that do the best will write something several times the length of the final assignment and then whittle it down to its essence for the final submission. 

## _<u>Written Component</u>_ 

The three components of this project are: (A) a written Five Forces Industry Analysis, (B) a written Positioning Analysis, and (C) Presentation of Problem and Recommendation of a Solution and final presentation and slides. You will get more information about the parameters of the final project separately. 

The first two written portions of the write-up should not exceed 3 pages (double spaced, 12-point font and 1 inch margins). We will only read the first 3 pages of text, so please stay within the page limit. The limit does not include a cover page or appendices, which you can use to provide charts, figures, or other background material, but these sections should be no longer than five (5) additional pages. The appendix is not a catch-all for anything that might be relevant, but is to be used to carefully support your points. Always include page numbers. 

## _<u>Presentation Component</u>_ 

The Presentation of Problem and Solution will take place on the last day of class and your team will present a key problem your client is facing and offer a solution to that problem. This presentation should include an executive summary slide and will also include the findings from your analyses where relevant. Each team member is encouraged to take a role in the final presentation. Additional guidelines (e.g., time) will be announced. Part of your participation grade in the course will involve asking questions to the other groups when they present.
