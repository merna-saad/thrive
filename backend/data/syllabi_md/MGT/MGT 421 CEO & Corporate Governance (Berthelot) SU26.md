### **COURSE DESCRIPTION** 

The Board of Directors, the CEO and Corporate Governance is designed as a 30-hour course to introduce students to the legal, political, and practical relationships that govern how businesses operate at the highest level of management.  Upon completing the course, students will have an understanding of the myriad relationships, agendas, and conflicts that come into play in managing, overseeing, or investing in a significant business enterprise as well as the ability to consider the appropriateness of the corporate governance structure within public or private business entities.   The course will include discussions of the evolution of the modern corporation and corporate governance; the structure, duties and responsibilities of boards of directors in primarily U.S. publicly-traded companies; the key areas of conflict between boards of directors, Chief Executive Officers, and shareholders as each pursues its individual mission; and, exercises in researching the sources to obtain information and evaluations on an individual company’s corporate governance practices as well as determining the “best practices” in corporate governance. 

All class reading materials, lecture slides, and written assignments will be available in Canvas.  All written assignments are to be submitted through Canvas. 

This class is a hybrid class, consisting of three in-person classes of four hours each (July 19, August 16, and September 13).  During each week between in-person classes, video lectures and other materials have been assigned to promote engagement and continuous learning.  Because our in-person classes are quite long – from 8:00 am to noon - we will try to take short breaks every 90 minutes or so.

It seems that almost every week a CEO is terminated, quits, or gets into trouble and this provides a great discussion point. I am counting on your help to make the class enjoyable and valuable.

### **COURSE OBJECTIVES** 

The objective of the course is to provide students with an understanding of the relationships among shareholders, managers, and boards and how to research and draw their own opinions on an organization’s corporate governance structure and practices. 

### **COURSE MATERIALS** 

Course materials will include a basic text: Corporate Governance (Fifth Edition) by Robert A.G. Monks and Nell Minow; and a Reader comprising short readings from: 

- Cases noted in the text can be accessed at: 

   - <u>http://bcs.wiley.com/he-bcs/Books?action=index&bcsId=6507&itemId=0470972599</u> 

- <u>The New York Stock Exchange Corporate Governance Listing Standards</u> 

- Articles on corporate governance topics, including strategy and the role of the CEO 

- Cases emphasizing corporate governance issues 

In addition, primarily using the Internet, students will be required to research various company specific corporate governance documents as well as current developments in the field as reported in the press and media and selected readings. 

Because this is a dynamic subject that is currently undergoing a substantial amount of legislative and regulatory review and change, additional readings may be assigned using freely accessible internet sites in order to capture the most current and relevant thoughts on the subject. 

Laptop computers will be used in each class session for online research. 

Presentation slides used in class will be posted on Canvas before each class. 

### **COURSE ASSIGNMENTS** 

Course assignments will include weekly readings and four written assignments of three to five questions on the topics covered. The written assignments will be considered as part of the class grade. Questions to be answered will be posted on Canvas at least one week prior to their due date.  Students’ written assignments will be submitted using the appropriate class drop box on Canvas prior to the commencement of the class for which they are due. 

The course will include a team research and evaluation project and paper to be completed and submitted in the last week of the term. The team project will involve small teams of students who will select a publicly traded company to use as their study subject in evaluating various areas of corporate governance.  Criteria for the selection of the company will include minimum market capitalization ($2 billion), U.S. filing status for at least two years on either the New York Stock Exchange, the American

Throughout the course teams will make presentations on various aspects of corporate governance.  At the conclusion of the course, each team will submit a presentation to the class as to how a client should vote on each issue in their company’s proxy statement along with a written paper providing more detailed analysis of the “whys” for their recommendations.  Details on the team project are posted on Canvas. 

This class is geared to maximum participation and interaction between students and the instructor.  As a result, class participation will have a significant bearing upon the final grade. 

### **GRADING CRITERIA** 

The course is a knowledge-based course that will focus on students developing an understanding of the environment and multiple parties involved in corporate governance so that students will be capable of developing an independent opinion as to the appropriateness of an organization’s governance structure and practices, including the ability to perform independent research on specific companies. ## **THE USE OF AI FOR RESEARCH IS ENCOURAGED IN THIS CLASS** 

The use of AI tools such as ChatGPT, Bing Chat, Bard, Claude, etc. is encouraged in this class. These tools have considerable potential to streamline and augment our work and you should use them for this class with the following caveats and conditions: 

1. <u>These tools should augment your work, not replace it. For instance, large language models can</u> improve the quality of your writing, identify new sources, provide high-level summaries of complex ideas or terms, and pressure test your arguments for logical inconsistencies. These tools can help you come up with new ideas, overcome writer’s block, or refine your original ideas. You should not use Generative AI tools to generate content that you simply put your name on. This constitutes an academic integrity violation, much like taking another individual’s work and misrepresenting it as your own.
