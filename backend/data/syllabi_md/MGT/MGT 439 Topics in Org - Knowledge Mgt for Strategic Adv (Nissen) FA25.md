## **DESCRIPTION** 

Even more than money, knowledge is the most important asset in your organization. Without timely knowledge to innovate powerfully, collaborate dynamically, and pivot preemptively, money is simply wasted on the weak organization waiting to fail. The problem is that our most powerful knowledge— associated with intelligent machines and people—is situated dynamically and must be managed actively to flow where, when and how it can be applied best. The organization that learns to harness its knowledge strategically can gain and maintain competitive advantage on that basis. In this course we learn how to compete through knowledge. We analyze strategy and differing bases of competitive advantage, and we discover how to mobilize the most powerful knowledge in the most appropriate manner. Our learning develops through three integrated instruction methods: 1) We integrate wellaccepted frameworks for understanding strategy with state-of-the-art methods for analyzing, visualizing and applying dynamic knowledge; 2) we interact intensively through group and class discussion to weave in both students’ and other organizations’ experiences; and 3) we apply our learning through case analysis. 

## **OBJECTIVES** 

Through participation in this course, you will learn to: 

- Analyze and characterize your organization’s competitive arena and context 

- Identify and differentiate the most powerful stocks and flows of organization knowledge 

- Amplify knowledge through technology and other sources of organization power 

- Devise the most promising bases for competitive advantage 

- Analyze, visualize, apply and accelerate dynamic knowledge 

- Harness knowledge strategically to gain and retain competitive advantage 

- Explore machine knowledge

## **Team Consulting Project** 

Students are to self-organize, work together in small teams (size TBD), and turn in one deliverable for the entire group. Please be inclusive in terms of encouraging students to join established teams, especially classmates whom you may not know well. Team diversity is a strength, and very few of us will have total control over our team composition in practice. The Instructor reserves the right to facilitate team formation. 

The assignment involves evaluating an operational organization in the field. Through the role of consultant in a strategic context, use course principles to evaluate the subject organization’s knowledge flows, diagnose any pathologies you detect, and recommend appropriate interventions. Submit a professional written report, and lead a professional classroom presentation. 

The report should include five sections: 1) introduction outlining the purpose and focal organization of the evaluation; 2) background summarizing key elements of knowledge management and strategy; 3) diagnosis of pathologies, accompanied by corresponding principles (e.g., from the textbook, induced from analysis of the case) used for evaluation; 4) recommended interventions that correspond closely to the pathologies and that identify corresponding principles used for evaluation; 5) summary of the factors and activities that you feel will be critical to successful implementation of your recommendations, along with suggested references for the organization leaders and managers to learn more about how to address and manage knowledge. 

The case analysis report should be roughly 10 – 15 pages in length (i.e., double spaced, 12 point font, 1- inch margins) including all text, tables, graphics and references. The report should be: 1) written in a professional manner; 2) based on factual observations and course principles; 3) persuasive; 4) constructive; and 5) informative. 

The case presentation will be the final class session. Each team should plan around 12 minutes for prepared remarks and 3 minutes for Q&A. PowerPoint or like presentation apps are appropriate, and students should plan to use either the classroom computer or their personal device. The presentation should be 1) professional; 2) engaging; 3) persuasive; 4) responsive; and 5) complete. 

## **Individual Presentation** 

For the Individual Presentation, students work individually and summarize very briefly one example of a contemporary organization that is competing effectively on the basis of its knowledge. The summary should describe the focal organization, its competitors and the competitive arena or environment. It should also outline the key knowledge used by the focal organization and explain how such knowledge enables competitive advantage. **Please plan one minute max** . **Exceeding your time will result in a deduction of points** . Simply present from your seat. No slides or props. The presentation should be 1) professional; 2) engaging; 3) current; 4) knowledge focused; and 5) competition focused. 

Note: Students are encouraged to use one or more large language (AI) models (e.g., Chat GPT, Bard, Claude) to facilitate this assignment.

## **COURSE POLICIES** 

Every student is expected to participate actively in every class session. Computer use is acceptable in class, except during student presentations. Cell phones should be silenced during class. 

Additionally, please note that this is a highly interactive course, in which all students exchange many ideas throughout every class session with the instructor and other students. It is important for students to feel relaxed and empowered to test even inchoate ideas in class. For this reason, class sessions will not be video recorded, as experience suggests that such recording is distracting. 

Finally, the use of artificial intelligence (AI) is encouraged in this class. This pertains in particular to the individual presentation and course project report. I expect normal standards of scholarship to be followed, for students to avoid plagiarization, for claimed facts to be checked, for text to be proofread, and for students to note which areas of the presentation and report have been generated in whole or in part through AI applications. I plan to use multiple AI detectors, and academic disciplinary issues may arise if you use AI and do not report it.
