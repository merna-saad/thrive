### **COURSE DESCRIPTION** 

This course provides students with external consulting experience that utilizes and builds upon the knowledge and skills acquired through work experience and in this course. The course enriches the Rady experiential learning component and provides an interdisciplinary approach to project execution and presentation. Projects are sponsored by and guided by clients from the business community. 

### **OBJECTIVES** 

- Gain a practical understanding of management consulting via hands-on experience working with a corporate client. 

- Gain an in-depth understanding of advanced management techniques, including project and client management, communication, and team dynamics. 

- Perfect use of business communications skills, oral and written, throughout the quarter, culminating in a final oral presentation and written executive summary. 

- Learn best practices for aligning client expectations with realistic project goals and objectives through effective communication and consensus building. 

- Create and deliver a well-thought-out and professionally executed final deliverable to the client

### **PROJECTS** 

A Rady Action Project is sponsored by a company or organization.  A typical project includes the following activities and can encompass a business problem that an MBA student can solve. 

1. Engaging with clients to clarify the problem – interviewing, questioning. 

   - a. Context 

   - b. Background 

   - c. Symptoms 

2. Structure the problem – fishbone diagram, etc. 

   - a. Determine Cause 

   - b. Revise if needed 

3. Analyze research –think of all the possibilities 

   - a. Develop solutions – how does it relate to the root cause 

4. Select a solution 

   - a. Feasibility 

   - b. Best Fit 

5. Ongoing communication with the client throughout the course, sharing your insights and solutions, and revising as needed 

Teams will consist of 3-6 students who receive project guidance from a project leader provided by the company and mentoring from faculty.  In addition, teams receive business communications coaching from industry and academic professionals. Students must bring their business skills and professionalism to the team to achieve project objectives. You represent the Rady brand alongside our sponsors, who are also employers interested in hiring UCSD students.

### **Written Assignments** 

- **Team Contract (G):** Outlines the roles and procedures the team will follow throughout the project. 

- **Project Charter (G)** – Outlines the current client situation (business opportunity or challenge), project goals, scope of work milestones and associated timelines, deliverables, risk assessment, and the basic terms and conditions under which the work will be performed. 

- **Work Plan(G)** – The detailed weekly breakdown of the scope of work milestones. WP is for internal use only and not shared with the client. You may input your project into Chat GPT and ask it to break down your tasks and milestones for your work plan. Although I will need names and details on the project plan. 

- **Client Status Reports (G)** – The client status report is due to the client 24 hours in advance. This document allows the client to stay up to date on the progress of the project.  You will also the submit  these reports weekly into canvas. 

- **Case - Assignments (I)-** Review the case _Skills for Tomorrow: A Management Team in Crisis._ Answer discussion board questions and respond to two peers. 

- **Discussion Posts –** Several discussion posts required throughout the quarter (Weeks 0,2,4,7)

### **AI STRATEGY FOR THE COURSE** 

The overarching philosophy is use AI as a junior analyst. 

**<u>Enhance, Don’t Replace</u>** – treat AI as an input to group collaboration. It serves to augment, not substitute, student critical thinking and faculty guidance. 

**<u>Final Accountability</u>** - Final responsibility for all deliverables rests with the student.  Think of AI as a junior analyst working for you- you are the lead partner. 

**<u>Ethical Guardrails</u>** -Transparency is paramount. Use of AI must be documented and cited in final reports. 

### **<u>RULES OF ENGAGEMENT (HOW SHOULD AI BE USED?)</u>** 

**<u>Hallucination Check</u>** <u>: Always verify outputs for errors or outdated information.  AI is a reasoning engine,</u> not a fact- checker 

**<u>Academic Integrity</u>** <u>: Plagiarism rules apply. Copy-paste without critical synthesis is prohibited.</u> **<u>Proper Citation</u>** <u>: Mandatory footnotes and bibliography entries for all AI-assisted work components.</u> **<u>Responsible Use</u>** <u>: Ensure sponsor data is never fed into public models without explicit security</u> confirmation.
