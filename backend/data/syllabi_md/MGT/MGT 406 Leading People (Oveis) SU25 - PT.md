**Christopher Oveis, Ph.D.** oveis@ucsd.edu; Office hours: by appointment TA: Arushi Srivastava (Arushi.Srivastava@rady.ucsd.edu) 

**Course description.** In this course, students will learn and develop key leadership skills. **Learning methodology.** Case analysis, articles, in-class exercises and discussion, written assignments. **Course objectives.** (1) Apply research-based knowledge to challenges facing managers and leaders; (2) Gain self-awareness of your current level of proficiency in key skills; (3) Accelerate development and progress toward one individualized leadership skill/goal; (4) Apply course concepts to your teams throughout the MBA program and beyond. 

**Course website.** rady.instructure.com (Canvas) 

**Course materials.** (1) Course Reader; (2) Additional materials provided on Canvas; (3) Slack **Attendance and technology.** Full on-time attendance and appropriate use of technology are required. _No AI-derived work (e.g., chatgpt) is permitted in this course._ 

**How to prep cases.** Read carefully; note important points you would like to discuss in class; put yourself in the shoes of the main protagonist(s), and consider how your decision making and actions would proceed under similar circumstances; consider analogies to your own work experiences, and prepare to draw these parallels in class and/or on Slack. 

**How to prep readings.** Consider how each article relates to its accompanying case; reflect on how the lessons from the article might apply to your current/future work environment. 

**How you are graded.** 40% participation, 35% PDP, 25% Team Paper. 

<u>Participation. Your class contributions are extremely important to your and your classmates’</u> learning. At the end of the course, your classmates (50%) and your professor (50%) will grade your participation. Your professor will consult with your TA and consider attendance and completion of ungraded assignments. Each of your classmates will respond to a prompt similar to the following: “Please choose a fair class participation grade for each student in your class. Consider the following factors as you see fit: How was the quality of the student’s contributions in full class discussions, small group discussions, and on Slack? Were their comments useful, on point, and/or controversial in a productive way? Did they share relevant experiences from their own background and work history? Did they listen carefully to their classmates, and engage them in thoughtful discussions? Were they wellprepared for class? To what extent did they provide value to your and others’ experience in the class?” <u>Personal Development Plan (PDP). An individually-completed plan for your leadership</u> development. It contains (A) a self portrait, (B) a goal, and (C) an action plan for obtaining your goal. Details are available on the course website.
