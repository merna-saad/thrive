## **DESCRIPTION** 

Why do some organizations succeed but others fail? And what can be done about it? The answers to these questions are consequential to your professional success because, regardless of career choice, people spend most of their lives as part of one organization or another. Building on lessons from prior leadership and team management courses, MGT 409 explores major determinants of a firm’s success or failure, and how to manage challenging organizational dynamics. Topics include social and professional networks, organizational networks and information exchange in markets, equity and inclusion, organizational culture and innovation, organizational design, corruption, organizational scandals, and fostering change in firms. 

**Instructional methods.** To translate theory into practice, this course is taught in an applied manner. Class sessions are structured around group exercises that allow you to engage with and experience relevant course concepts firsthand. Exercises are followed by instructorfacilitated group discussions of illustrative case studies and evidence-based insights generated by behavioral and management scientists, which contextualize your experiences and ground course concepts in real organizational settings. Due to the highly condensed nature of this class, there is also time built-in to work on a mini capstone final project that will help you apply what you learn in real time. 

## **OBJECTIVES** 

The central learning objective is to provide you with an organizational strategy toolkit, which can be used throughout your career. By the end of this course, you will be able to: 

- Identify important characteristics of your social and professional network and understand how to leverage your network to benefit your career and your firm 

- Identify the major contributing factors to an organization’s success or failure, and critically analyze/diagnose these processes in real organizations 

- Develop evidence-based solutions to challenging organizational dynamics and effectively communicate your insights and strategies

3. Large language models have documented issues with hallucination and inaccuracy (e.g., generating fake citations, failing logical arguments, misrepresenting sources, etc.). You <u>are fully responsible for the content you submit in your assignments and will be graded</u> accordingly. 

4. Overreliance on generative AI tools, specifically in the form of using it to “think” for you, is shown to be associated with brain atrophy (see MIT Media Lab study here). The way to avoid this is to engage in your own critical thinking first and then use generative AI tools to help refine your original ideas. Keep this in mind!

**Group Project: Real World Case Analysis** (group grade). To demonstrate your ability to apply course concepts and communicate evidence-based solutions, you will work on a team to conduct a postmortem analysis of a real organizational challenge or failure event and propose a do-over strategy. 

_*** I recognize this class schedule is a tight timeline for group projects! Because students in this class can come from multiple cohorts, teams assignments will be based on study groups but students from other cohorts will also be randomly assigned. Each team can pick their own topic. I recommend using the following steps as a guide for approaching this group project: 

1. **Pick an organizational failure event or challenge that you find interesting** . Feel free to interpret this broadly. Think about a firm that has faced and failed to overcome a competitive challenge, failed in a major or meaningful way, or was embroiled in a scandal. History is full of organizational failures, giving you many possible topics. There is a list of possible topics on Canvas to give you inspiration, but you are not limited to this list. All topics are subject to my final approval. The constraints on topic selection are: 

   - It must be an organizational failure and not a single person’s failure. 

   - It cannot already be covered in a case study discussed in class (see schedule at the end of the syllabus for a list of cases being used in class). 

   - The topic must be well enough known that there is sufficient evidence to analyze. 

   - • No two groups can do the same topic. Once you identify your topic, put it on the sign-up list on Canvas to claim it. 

Keep in mind that many organizational failures have had serious consequences for people’s lives and livelihoods. Please be respectful of this when selecting your topic and conducting your analysis. 

2. **Figure out what happened, why it happened, and what you would have done differently** . I recommend reviewing the final project grading rubric available on Canvas to understand how your project will be evaluated. A compelling postmortem analysis should address the following and be supported by reliable evidence:
