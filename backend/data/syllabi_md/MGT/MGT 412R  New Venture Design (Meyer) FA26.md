# COURSE DESCRIPTION 

This course is the foundational elective to begin shaping your MBA for a career based upon Innovation, Design, or Entrepreneurship. Along with _MGT 452: New Product Development_ , it forms the core of the Certificate in Design & Innovation. For those looking to fully prepare their startup to launch post-MBA, it is a prerequisite for _MGT 415: New Venture Development._ 

The course will provide experience in transforming an understanding of user need, a company’s strategic intent, and the scientific or technological innovation to which the company has access, into viable new lines of business or ventures that bring desirable and profitable new offerings to the world. The course will provide an introduction to the user-based, strategic and operational aspects of sparking an innovation and developing it into a product or service that people will love, as a competitive and sustainable business. 

Traditional business planning has generally approached this challenge in a top-down manner, in the sense of being driven by the company’s motivations, capabilities, or vision of the future. The newer practice of Design Thinking frames the process as one driven from below. Vital insights as to people’s needs and wants are gathered through primary research, and the company takes its direction from the people it serves. 

In actual practice, successful companies pursue a hybrid top-down and bottom-up approach, though often without framing this as an explicit process, or even realizing that they are doing so. We will learn the tools that help do this, explore their utility and boundaries, and place them in the context of an overarching framework. 

The course follows a “hybrid” online + in-person format, with three online modules in Canvas, and three in-person class sessions at Rady, alternating online / in-person. Additionally, teams will meet individually with the instructor twice, once between the first and second in-person sessions and again between the second and third in-person sessions, for focused coaching and critique as they develop their concepts and final presentations. 

Course materials are a mix of case studies, readings, multimedia, and practical exercises. Online assignments are meant to provide the flexibility for students and teams to adapt workflow to their own 



schedules, with due dates to ensure that materials are available as needed for peer feedback, instructor feedback, and whole-class discussion. 

The peer feedback and whole-class discussion assignments are a vital part of the learning process, because, as managers after graduation, you will need to evaluate and critique the work of others on your team and across the organization, as well as contributing your own work. Active participation is vital, as much of this can only be learned by doing. 

There is a final project, in which student teams will develop a concept that forms the basis of a new line of business, and the accompanying explanation of why this is a compelling offering from the customer’s perspective and a compelling opportunity from the business’ perspective. We will help you initiate, develop, and refine your concept using the practical exercises throughout the course, and you will present your final concept pitch to the entire class in the third in-person session. 

For those students pursuing a startup idea, the team you form and concept you explore this quarter does not need to continue into the Winter _New Venture Development_ course - many students discover better opportunities and form stronger teams as a result of their experience in this course, and you are absolutely free to pursue those. Others find value in learning the essential tools using a “practice” idea – one free from the preconceptions and assumptions they may have accumulated. 

# COURSE OBJECTIVES 

New Venture Design is an exercise in learning by practical application. This course will ground your approach in the realities of market and user need, and sharpen your ability to craft and articulate both opportunity and solution. By the end of this course, you will be able to: 

1. Analyze the factors that focus a company’s innovation and new offering development efforts, to articulate a best focus for these efforts, along with a supporting rationale. 

2. Perform actual user and market research, analyze the findings, and synthesize to uncover and articulate deep insight as to the users’ needs. Combine this with other relevant data to highlight opportunities for new offerings. 

3. Construct and articulate viable and compelling positioning for the new offerings and concepts, in order to clarify, communicate, and guide further work. 

4. Prototype, test, and refine concepts, to create real value for your customer and to deliver that value through an excellent customer experience. 

5. Craft and present a compelling business case, expressing the opportunity, concept, and rationale with an appropriate level of tangibility and specificity. 

In summary, this course will refresh, refine, and extend the knowledge, skills, and ability gained in the core curriculum, with an emphasis on uncovering unique insights and opportunities that can drive the creation of new lines of business. The course will push you to go beyond superficial recognitions or merely interesting ideas, and craft offerings that are both compelling and viable.

# Artificial Intelligence (AI) Policy and Approach 

Generative AI is a disruptive force in both business and education. While many choose to focus on the negative aspects of disruption, leaders of Design and Innovation teams recognize that the disruption of established systems and competitive dynamics also creates opportunities for those who are able to harness the new tools and master the new landscapes created as a result. This is the essence of the concept “Disruptive Innovation” at the heart of Clayton Christensen’s seminal work _The Innovator’s Dilemma_ . 

We also know from the history of technological advances in design and manufacturing that new tools tend to improve both quality and speed of work for the thoughtful user, and shift the thoughtful adopter’s focus to the higher-value creative and intellectual tasks. Thoughtless (and non-) adopters become obsolete. 

The assignments in this course do not lend themselves to simply “having ChatGPT do it;” I know this from experiments we have run in previous quarters of this course. There are, however, elements that we can learn to accomplish better or faster by treating generative AI as an assistant to our work, which further helps us develop our ability to critique and guide innovation work, critical managerial skills. There are also examples of good process that a custom agent can model for us to help us learn to use good process. You will have an opportunity to experience this firsthand through exercises during our in-person sessions and through custom agents I have created for your use in this course, such as the _NPD Ideation Assistant_ . 

To help us advance our ability to use AI as an effective tool and to identify ineffective uses of AI, I allow its use in this course under the following conditions: Cite and Critique . 

Cite : Identify which AI tool you used, and for what task(s). Save and forward the prompt and output. 

Critique : Give your assessment of how helpful the tool was to you and your team. Identify how it could have been more helpful. I will synthesize these learnings for the class as a whole. 

This applies both to any tools you discover and to the custom tools and agents I supply for your use.
