## **DESCRIPTION** 

Why do some organizations succeed but others fail? And what can be done about it? The answers to these questions are consequential to your professional success because, regardless of career choice, people spend most of their lives as part of one organization or another. Building on lessons from prior leadership and team management courses, MGT 409 explores major determinants of a firm’s success or failure, and how to manage challenging organizational dynamics. Topics include social and professional networks, organizational networks and information exchange in markets, equity and inclusion, organizational culture and innovation, organizational design, corruption, organizational scandals, and fostering change in firms. 

**Instructional methods.** To translate theory into practice, this course is taught in an applied manner. Class sessions are structured around group exercises that allow you to engage with and experience relevant course concepts firsthand. Exercises are followed by instructorfacilitated group discussions of illustrative case studies and evidence-based insights generated by behavioral and management scientists, which contextualize your experiences and ground course concepts in real organizational settings. 

## **OBJECTIVES** 

The central learning objective is to provide you with an organizational strategy toolkit, which can be used throughout your career. By the end of this course, you will be able to: 

- Identify important characteristics of your social and professional network and understand how to leverage your network to benefit your career and your firm 

- Identify the major contributing factors to an organization’s success or failure, and critically analyze/diagnose these processes in real organizations 

- Develop evidence-based solutions to challenging organizational dynamics and effectively communicate your insights and strategies

You can incorporate traditional and anecdotal evidence like personal experiences, learnings from this class or other courses, advice from peers or mentors, evidence from reliable sources (e.g., news articles, articles written by experts, white papers), etc.

- Responses should be concise and generative for in-class discussions (max of 300 words in total). Excessively long responses will not receive high scores.

**Group Project: Real World Case Analysis** (group grade, 60 pts). To demonstrate your ability to apply course concepts and communicate evidence-based solutions, you will work on a team to conduct a postmortem analysis of a real organizational challenge or failure event and propose a do-over strategy. This final project includes a written memo and an in-class presentation. Your team will be your study group, and each team can pick their topic. I recommend using the following steps as a guide for approaching this group project: 

1. **Pick an organizational failure event or challenge that you find interesting** . Feel free to interpret this broadly. Think about a firm that has faced and failed to overcome a competitive challenge, failed in a major or meaningful way, or was embroiled in a scandal. History is full of organizational failures, giving you many possible topics. There is a list of possible topics on Canvas to give you inspiration, but you are not limited to this list. All topics are subject to my final approval. The constraints on topic selection are: 

   - It must be an organizational failure and not a single person’s failure. 

   - It cannot already be covered in a case study discussed in class (see schedule at the end of the syllabus for a list of cases being used in class). 

   - The topic must be well enough known that there is sufficient evidence to analyze. 

   - • No two groups can do the same topic. Once you identify your topic, put it on the sign-up list on Canvas to claim it. 

Keep in mind that many organizational failures have had serious consequences for people’s lives and livelihoods. Please be respectful of this when selecting your topic and conducting your analysis. 

2. **Figure out what happened, why it happened, and what you would have done differently** . I recommend reviewing the final project grading rubric available on Canvas to understand how your project will be evaluated. A compelling postmortem analysis should address the following and be supported by reliable evidence: 

   - Description/background context: What happened and why is it important for us to understand? 

   - Analysis: What were the major contributing factors or catalysts to this failure event, and why?

   - Proposed do-over strategy: Imagine that you have gone back in time and the firm has hired your team as consultants to help them navigate this challenge or failure event in real time. You are encouraged to approach this creatively. For instance, depending on the type of failure event, you could imagine your team was hired just before the failure occurred (i.e., you focus on preventing the failure) or after a failure becomes public (i.e., you focus on improving the firm’s post-scandal response). What are your strategic recommendations for how the firm should handle this situation, or could have avoided it altogether? Why will your proposed strategy be successful? 

3. **Tell us about your insights** . Write up your analysis and proposed do-over strategy in a memo and present your findings to class. 

<u>Written memo: This is a report of your evidence, analysis, insights, and do-over strategy.</u> You can be creative with formatting, and I recommend thinking about the most compelling, informative, and persuasive structure to present this information to the firm’s leadership. The memo should not be longer than 2,000 words (equivalent of ~8 pgs double spaced with 12-pt font). Appendices with supplementary evidence, tables/figures, references, brief abstracts, and title pages are excluded from the word count. Excessively long memos will not receive high scores. 

<u>In-class presentation: Your group will have 10-15 minutes during Class 10 to present</u> your findings to your classmates (additional details provided in class). These presentations are intended to be overviews and are not expected to cover everything that is in your written memo. Think critically about what is most important. 

_Note: You will be learning course content relevant to your project through Class 9, which is only a few days before the final presentations. I understand this is a time crunch and do not expect Class 9 content to be integrated into the presentation (but you are welcome to if you’d like). But you are given adequate time to integrate any/all relevant course concepts for the written memo._
