## **COURSE DESCRIPTION** 

In today’s increasingly digital economy, the availability of data is widespread and business success can depend on smart, capable, and clever use of this data. Business analytics now drives ticket pricing, retail assortments, movie recommendations, digital marketing campaigns, healthcare scheduling, crime fighting, social media investments, capacity decisions and more. Today’s companies generate an immense volume of transactional data, capturing trillions of bytes of information about their customers, suppliers, and operations. Millions of networked sensors are also embedded in the physical world in devices such as computers, mobile phones, smart energy meters, automobiles and industrial machines. Further, in a digitized world, consumers communicate, browse, buy, share, and search, creating extensive trails of valuable data. 

Hence, analytics — the use of data with statistical and quantitative models to make better, data-driven business decisions — has become important to gain competitive advantages. With all of these different sources of data ranging from online sensors, email campaigns, to physical sensors, business analytics is pervasive in any size business. 

This importance of analytics means that all managers should be comfortable discussing, assessing, and effectively conducting their own analyses. There is a significant shortage (and significant demand!) of business analysts and project managers who are adept at effectively utilizing analytics to create substantial value to an organization by solving impactful business problems. 

This course introduces students to the foundations of business analytics. It teaches managers how to be comfortable exploring their data, how to create and use models to make better decisions in complex settings, and how to use statistics to better understand their business setting. ## **LEARNING OBJECTIVES** 

There are three main Units we cover in this course. Each Unit teaches you tools to answer key data- driven and quantitative questions. Each of the three units center around a central theme of addressing and studying uncertainty. Data and business analytics allows us to better understand and deal with uncertainty. 

### **Unit 1: Randomness and Uncertainty** 

- Decisions when facing uncertain and probabilistic (“random”) future outcomes. 

- Test the important of our assumptions in our models (“sensitivity analysis”) 

- Modeling randomness to understand what risks and potential outcomes you face. 

- • Understanding why data gives us confidence and interpreting such confidence. • Making evidence-based business decisions (“hypothesis testing”). 

- Is the evidence sufficient to convince us? Or is it just noise? [Key business question.] 

### **Unit 2: Prediction and modeling relationships** 

Unit 1 primarily focuses on studying individual random variables. Unit 2 expands our tools to model relationships between multiple variables. 

- Use data to create predictions for the future (“regression”). 

- Use regression to interpret the relationships between data. o Understand the limitations of regression and how to address those limitations. 

   - Understand the limitations of causality interpretation. 

- Use simulation to predict complex future outcomes (such as long-tail insurance risks). 

### **Unit 3: Optimization** 

In optimization, we transition from modeling random outcomes to optimizing decisions, such as how many resources to allocate to different sectors or investments. Each use of resources has tradeoffs, which we learn to use tools to quantitatively model and consider. 

- Simulating possible futures, and using this simulation to make the optimal decision. 

- • Formalizing the resource tradeoffs you face in a business into an optimization model. 

- Using optimization model to understand the best use of many scarce resources, when faced with many tradeoffs. 

All of these are KEY ways businesses and managers must think about their problems. This course will give you ample experience in these fundamental ways of quantitative thinking.

## **RADIANT TOOL** 

The central tool we use in this course to perform analysis is Radiant. 

The modern use of statistics and analytics heavily relies on statistical software to conduct analyses. “R” is one of the most famous and is heavily used throughout all companies. However, using R requires being comfortable with coding first. 

To best teach analytical thinking, we use Radiant to give you the power of exploring your data, visualizing it, and seeing analysis without needing to code. Radiant is built on-top of R, meaning it is free and it can be used at any business that can use R — which again, almost all modern analytics teams support the use of R. 

Lastly and most importantly, Radiant is a valuable resume piece. This course will not only give you comfort with “data visualization”, “business analytics”, “regression analysis”, and “linear optimization” — you will also have experience with “R statistical analysis (via the Radiant interface)”.

### **_GROUP CASES_** 

There are 6 group cases due this course in Weeks 3, 4, 5, 6, 8, and 10. 

- These group cases consist of a slightly more in-depth analysis in a specific business and industry setting. The goal of each case is for you to discuss the case in detail with your team, figure out which analyses are useful to apply, and then together agree upon recommendations. These recommendations should be backed up by solid arguments, evidence, and mathematical or data analysis as needed. 

- The analysis will be slightly more difficult, but the grading of these cases will also focus on how you convey your analysis and findings. Communicating analyses is a very valuable aspect of business analytics. It takes practice to balance explaining analytics in-depth without overloading detail. 

- Each case will require a ~2 page memo summarizing your analyses, along with an Appendix including the detailed analyses. The memo should be written in a

## **AI + CHATGPT POLICY** 

ChatGPT and large-language AI models have become popular in the past couple years. They can answer simple statistical questions. Modern statistical analysts and programmers can use these, however, they must also be wary that they can make simple mistakes too. 

If you find it helpful, you are welcome to try to use ChatGPT to offer advice or additional support on your homework, cases, and midterm projects. However, you must ultimately do the work on your own in Radiant and show the supporting evidence. And for the case writing, your group is expected to write the executive summary report and include analysis
