

# **MGT 403 Business Analytics for Managers** 

Fall 2025 

## **Prof. Robert Montgomery** 

## **DESCRIPTION** 

This course provides a foundation for business analytics, and is intended for students of all backgrounds. Students will gain experience processing data, constructing mathematical models to improve decision making, and using statistics to gain business insights. Data analytical fluency has become an essential skill for any manager. As such, we will work extremely hard to ensure students have all the support and resources they need to succeed in this course. This course will also serve as a bridge to higher level analytics courses within The Rady School of Management. 

The course will be comprised of three units: Statistic & Uncertainty, Decision Analysis, and Optimization. The first unit will focus on data visualization and probability, spending a significant amount of time on regression analysis. The second unit will carry forward the notion of uncertainty presented in the first unit, and focus on modeling techniques for using data to make better decisions under uncertainty. We will emphasize interpretating model results and will spend two weeks working on simulation approaches. The final unit will introduce optimization models, with the objective of providing an understanding of the capabilities of optimization and recognizing opportunities to deploy such models. 

To allow students to focus on the key concepts of the course, the course is built around the Radiant Tool. Radiant is a data processing and visualization tool that does not require coding, while operating within the R environment (which is free to use). 

## **OBJECTIVES** 

At the close of this course, you will be able to: 

- Read and interpret business analytics reports 

- Produce data visualizations using Radiant software 

- Understand how to use data to improve operations and decision making 

- Build a wide array of models to help make decisions under uncertainty 

- Synthesize and communicate the important findings from modeling and data analysis

## **ASSIGNMENTS** 

There will be eight individual assignments, five group cases, and a midterm project. 

The eight individual assignments will be submitted as quizzes in Canvas. You will have the opportunity to take each of these quizzes twice. This will allow you to see which questions you got wrong on the first attempt and retry these questions for learning purposes. You must include your handwritten and/or Radiant work as part of the submission. Teaching assistants will check to ensure students have submitted this work. If you collaborated with classmates, please indicate who you worked with at the top of your work. All students must submit their own work. 

The five group cases will require more in-depth analysis and provide an opportunity to practice communicating your analyses and findings. We will cover expectations and examples for these assignments in the first week of the course. 

The midterm project will be an opportunity for you and your group to conduct an in-depth analysis of a dataset, write a report of your findings, and present a summary to the class. The presentations will take place during week 6. You will be expected to incorporate the topics covered in weeks 1-4 in the report. The instructor will provide several datasets that students can use, but encourages students to find their own datasets that interest them, if this option is available to them. More information will be provided in the first two weeks of the class.
