# **COURSE OBJECTIVES** 

Our class focuses on the topic of Contextual Growth and Entrepreneurship, which makes students reflect on their current context and tailor their growth and entrepreneurship path, thereby accelerating their career trajectory. The course exposes students to a variety of options on the risk/reward spectrum, including Acquisition Entrepreneurship, Side-hustles, Scalable Entrepreneurship, Corporate Entrepreneurship, and Angel/Venture Investing. Students develop a Personal Entrepreneurial Plan (PEP) and/or a business pitch deck, as well as engage in a multiweek simulation. The simulation, originally developed at Stanford University, helps us experience the dynamics of a venture lifecycle and challenges us to raise money and strategically invest in growth while building wealth for the founders and stakeholders. 

Through a combination of real-life venture case studies and a venture simulation, we learn and immerse ourselves on the topics of venture innovation, business model development, venture financing, and growth strategy.   In addition, we will learn first-hand from distinguished alumni/entrepreneurs and venture capitalists in the life-sciences and technology industries their journey of juggling a new venture and developing/executing a business model while working full-time and learning the economics of the industry to build a thriving new venture. The examples include companies that went from an Idea to an IPO to others that are still well into their journey towards major milestones. Our goal is to develop the creative, strategic and critical thinking skills characteristic of an innovative, opportunity-driven, and entrepreneurial manager. We consider different types of startup/enterprises in the life sciences and information technology industries as well as lifestyle/service businesses. We also consider other approaches such as buying your way into entrepreneurship. The course may help students get a firmer grasp of the nuances and trade-offs, find their innovation sweetspot, and develop their professional growth plan in an established or start-up setting. We challenge students to build on their personal leadership plan to chart out their entrepreneurial/growth trajectory for the medium time horizon.

<u>Class Engagement/Participation (20%):</u> 

Your class participation (contribution to class discussions) will count for 20% of the class grade. You are expected to be an active participant throughout the entire class and to contribute to the quality of the discussion. Please note that the frequency (i.e., the quantity) of your interventions in class is not a key criterion for effective class contribution. Some criteria used to evaluate class contribution are as follows: 

1. Is the participant deeply engaged in class? 

2. Is the participant a good listener? Are the points made relevant to the discussion? Are they linked to the comments of others? Do the comments show evidence of analysis of the case? 

3. Is there willingness to test new ideas, or are all comments "safe"? (For example, repetition of case facts without analysis and conclusions). 

4. Do comments clarify or build upon the important aspects of earlier comments and lead to a clearer understanding of the case? 

Note: You are expected to attend EVERY class; you are responsible for the material covered in class whether or not you attend. However, I realize that despite your best efforts you might have be forced to miss a class due to unforeseen emergencies. If you must miss a class, you may provide a one-page written evaluation of the material or case to be covered (contact us for details of specific assignments). This assignment should be e-mailed in advance of class for planned absences and by the BEGINNING of the following class for emergencies.

Few minutes of class sessions will be allocated to an in-class Canvas quiz based on a case assigned for that class.  These are to be taken without any external/AI guidance and would be mostly administered using a lockdown browser. There will be four quizzes based on case studies.
