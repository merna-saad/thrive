## **DESCRIPTION** 

The course will draw on the principals from corporate finance and investments and apply them to the context of real estate markets in their various shapes and forms (private vs. public markets; debt vs. equity). However, we may at times make reference to the complementary fields of real estate law, public and urban economics, design and architecture, and the economics of inequality. 

## **OBJECTIVES** 

While the study of real estate can be undertaken from many different perspectives, we will choose the value perspective as our major theme. What is the value of a particular asset? This question is central to all real estate decision making: acquiring a property, mortgage underwriting, real estate development, lease vs. buy decisions, to name a few. Additionally, the course will focus on commercial real estate (CRE) assets. Office, Multi-family Residential, Retail and Industrial are the four main types of commercial real estate. The CRE landscape also encompasses assets such as hotels, leisure facilities, data centers, cell-phone towers, among others. Finally, we will address historical trends in real estate, including the legacy of residential segregation, redlining, and reverse-redlining. 

Fundamentals of Real Estate Analysis thus has the following objectives: 

To develop students’ awareness of a fundamental value-based framework—both its strengths and weaknesses—from which to analyze a key set of real estate decisions. 

To heighten students’ awareness of macroeconomic and local demand and supply factors which drive value. 

To increase students literacy of the historical drivers of urban patterns and the role of public institutions in driving or correcting historical patterns. 

To introduce students to financial modelling techniques in real estate investment analysis. To understand the impact of critical assumptions (model inputs) on valuation and risk metrics (model outputs). Consequently, to make students aware of the strengths and weaknesses of these widely used techniques, and help them develop an intuition for, and a critical eye towards, these models. 

To develop an understanding of risk and its relationship to return in the context of real estate investments. Additionally, to understand how risk interacts with the financing and capital structure of real estate investments.

## **USE OF ARTIFICIAL INTELLIGENCE (AI)** 

The use of Artificial Intelligence (“AI”) tools such as ChatGPT, Bing Chat, Bard, Claude, and similar systems is permitted in this course. These tools may be used to support learning, including improving writing quality, clarifying complex ideas, identifying potential sources, and refining arguments. 

AI tools should augment, not replace, independent thinking and original work. Submitting AI-generated content as one’s own constitutes an academic integrity violation, comparable to misrepresenting another person’s work. Students are expected to demonstrate their own understanding of course material. 

Students may use AI tools for homework assignments. However, reliance on AI as a substitute for mastering core concepts is strongly discouraged. Most variation in course grades is determined by inperson examinations, which assess students’ independent understanding of fundamental concepts. Students who do not develop this understanding will perform poorly on the exam regardless of AI use during the semester. 

I assume all students have access to and will use generative AI. Accordingly, work that does not exceed the baseline quality readily produced by AI should expect a low grade. Students are fully responsible for the accuracy, originality, and integrity of all submitted work, including verifying AI-generated content.
