# **COURSE  DESCRIPTION:** 

Supply/Value Chains are among the most discussed topics of today at the business, societal and policy levels. In the highly competitive, volatile, uncertain, complex, and ambiguous (VUCA) international environment that we operate in, companies do not compete. _Supply chains and industries compete. And countries compete through their supply chains._ It is imperative, therefore, that managers, policy makers and students understand global supply chains, their operations, the key decisions and trade-offs and the events that impact them. 

The course addresses the new issues and environment of the Global Supply Chain, the impacts of technology and people, and the new imperatives and practices for companies and governments. It is a critical course for executives, managers, policy-makers and students in all the major functions of business and policy across all industrial sectors. 

The supply chain is the most significant and critical component of business and national security, and accounts for the bulk of the material, people, cash and value-added in an organization. Recent events –  trade wars and tariffs, the pandemic, wars, major international crises and extreme weather events have highlighted the critical role that the supply chains play in business, global trade, the world economy,  as well as in national security and societal wellbeing. 

The supply chain involves the flow of materials, cash, information and processes from the source of raw materials to end customers... and back. Its objectives are multiple and complex and include maximizing shareholder value (and stakeholder interests) while minimizing total

This “holistic”, multi-faceted and “End-to-End” view of the supply/value chain incorporates Critical Thinking and is very different from the traditional functional approach. It is geared towards understanding the modern supply chain in the modern environment. 

The course will be taught using a combination of several learning approaches – classroom lectures, case analyses and projects. It will provide an overview of supply chains and their key functions, the economic environment, the financial impacts and levers, while addressing the major challenges in managing an efficient supply chain.

# **COURSE LEARNING OUTCOMES** 

Upon completing this course, students will obtain an understanding of: 

- the End-to-End (E2E) supply chain, the critical component and set of functions in global business. 

- the economic and business drivers of supply chain strategy and structure 

- demand management, the needs of customers and the customer life cycle experience (CX) 

- supply and sourcing imperatives and strategies, and matching demand and supply 

- leading practices, core components and strategic aspects of today’s supply chains, including risk, resilience, and sustainability, and their drivers and enablers– technology and talent. 

- the key decisions, trade-offs that must be made in today’s global, fragile, uncertain, and technologically driven supply chains. 

The course will prepare students to leave this class armed with an understanding of today’s issues, what’s needed, impacts and decisions, and to be able to confidently articulate these with C-level executives and governmental leaders.

# **Faculty: Christopher Gopal, PhD.** 

Dr. Gopal has over 40 years of experience in global supply chain & operations strategy, execution, and technology in a career that has encompassed industry executive management, consulting and executive education.
