## MGT

### MGT 403/R Business Analytics for Managers

In today’s increasingly global and competitive economy, businesses are striving to use analytics (i.e., the use of data, together with statistical and quantitative models, to make better, data-driven business decisions) to gain competitive advantage. Yet, there is still a significant shortage of business analysts and project managers who are adept at effectively utilizing analytics to create substantial value to an organization by solving impactful business problems. The use of business analytics has exploded and become pervasive in small and large businesses. Business analytics now drives ticket pricing, retail assortments, movie recommendations, digital marketing campaigns, healthcare scheduling, crime fighting, social media investments, capacity decisions and more. Today, companies generate an immense volume of transactional data, capturing trillions of bytes of information about their customers, suppliers, and operations. Millions of networked sensors are embedded in the physical world in devices such as computers, mobile phones, smart energy meters, automobiles and industrial machines that sense, create, and communicate data. In a digitized world, consumers communicate, browse, buy, share, and search, creating extensive trails of valuable data. In this course, students are introduced to the foundations of business analytics. The course is designed to teach business managers how to use data and models to make better decisions in complex business settings. Note that a good outcome does not imply a good decision. A good outcome may simply be a matter of luck, and, conversely, a bad outcome is not always evidence of a bad decision. Students will develop sound reasoning skills and learn how to utilize information to arrive at good decisions.

### MGT 404/R Accounting

This course gives a graduate-level introduction to financial accounting. I do not assume any prior knowledge of accounting on your part but expect you to have a basic understanding of business economics and elementary algebra. We will study the conceptual foundations and the mechanical structure of accounting systems, as well as the economics of the business transactions and events they operate on in practice. The goal is to make you informed, intelligent users of accounting information. The course covers the standard financial statements (balance sheet, income statement, and cash flow statement), the functions of the underlying ledger system, and the economic foundations of accounting standards and principles and their application in practice.  The course is broadly organized into two modules. In the first part of the course, we will study the institutions of accounting, the structure of financial reports, and the mechanical functioning of the standard, double-entry ledger system. In the second part, we will apply the system to the major economic events and transactions that businesses encounter in practice, including, among others, the recognition of revenue, the treatment of long-term capital assets, and the recognition of various liabilities.

### MGT 405/R Managerial Economics

This course is an introduction to the use of microeconomics in managerial decision-making. We examine how the optimal strategy of a firm is impacted by the structure of the market, the competitive and regulatory constraints, and the structure of information. Topics include supply and demand analysis, price discrimination techniques, contractual relationships, and asymmetric information. Throughout we highlight the importance of marginal analysis in decisions. All the subfields of management education (marketing, finance, organizational behavior, operations,

information systems, accounting, and strategy) deal in some way with how businesses and other organizations work to allocate scarce resources among competing needs. Because of this, economics can serve as a unifying framework. I will show you connections between economics and many of the other courses you will take as part of your MBA.

### MGT 412/R New Venture Design

This course is the foundational elective to begin shaping your MBA for a career based upon Innovation, Design, or Entrepreneurship. Along with MGT 452: New Product Development, it forms the core of the Certificate in Design & Innovation. For those looking to fully prepare their startup to launch post-MBA, it is a prerequisite for MGT 415: New Venture Development.

The course will provide experience in transforming an understanding of user need, a company’s strategic intent, and the scientific or technological innovation to which the company has access, into viable new lines of business or ventures that bring desirable and profitable new offerings to the world. The course will provide an introduction to the user-based, strategic and operational aspects of sparking an innovation and developing it into a product or service that people will love, as a competitive and sustainable business.

Traditional business planning has generally approached this challenge in a top-down manner, in the sense of being driven by the company’s motivations, capabilities, or vision of the future. The newer practice of Design Thinking frames the process as one driven from below. Vital insights as to people’s needs and wants are gathered through primary research, and the company takes its direction from the people it serves. In actual practice, successful companies pursue a hybrid top-down and bottom-up approach, though often without framing this as an explicit process, or even realizing that they are doing so. We will learn the tools that help do this, explore their utility and boundaries, and place them in the context of an overarching framework.

The course follows a “hybrid” online + in-person format, with four online modules in Canvas, and four in-person class sessions at Rady, alternating online / in-person. Additionally, teams will meet individually with the instructor for focused coaching and assistance as they develop their concepts and final presentations. Course materials are a mix of case studies, non-business-text readings, multimedia, and practical exercises.

Online assignments are meant to provide the flexibility for students and teams to adapt workflow to their own schedules, with due dates to ensure that materials are available as needed for peer feedback and whole-class discussion.

The peer feedback and whole-class discussion assignments are a vital part of the learning process, because, as managers after graduation, you will need to evaluate and critique the work of others on your team and across the organization, as well as contributing your own work. Active participation is vital, as much of this can only be learned by doing.

There is a final project, in which student teams will develop a concept that forms the basis of a new line of business, and the accompanying explanation of why this is a compelling offering from the customer’s perspective and a compelling opportunity from the business’ perspective. We will help you initiate, develop, and refine your concept using the practical exercises throughout the course, and you will present your final concept pitch to the entire class in the fourth in-person session.

For those students pursuing a startup idea, the team you form and concept you explore this quarter does not need to continue into the Winter New Venture Development course - many students discover better opportunities and form stronger teams as a result of their experience in this course, and you are absolutely free to pursue those. Others find value in learning the essential tools using a “practice” idea – one free from the preconceptions and assumptions they may have accumulated.

### MGT 440 Supply Chain Cost Management Strategy

The current global economic uncertainty has posed unprecedented challenges for companies across industries. While some focus on survival, world class companies look at opportunities to gain market share through the implementation of a sound business strategy. As these companies re-examine what will make them competitive, technology, quality, service, delivery and global presence will be taken for granted. Interdependencies within a supply chain have become increasingly prevalent and critical to a firm’s success. In today’s marketplace, the term competition has expanded beyond rivalries between organizations to battles between integrated supply chains. Winners will be those that can provide leading edge technology, world class quality, exceptional service, and on time delivery at the lowest total cost. ### MGT 446 Spreadsheet Modeling

This course is designed to provide students with knowledge and techniques to apply management science principles and spreadsheet modeling to support decision-making under various business scenarios. Given the complex nature of innovation and technological development, accelerated by advancement of information technology and globalization, making right decisions under uncertainty has become critical for modern businesses in nearly any industry sector. Moreover, we are entering the new era of Industry 4.0, in which manufacturing companies adopt smart manufacturing which marries physical production and operations with information technology to increase productivity through a better-connected ecosystem. Thus, computer-aided decision-making is a very important skill for business managers these days. In this course, we learn the fundamentals of management science, analytical modeling, and problem solving using spreadsheets.

While this course will cover decision problems in various business contexts, emphasis will be on the operations and technology. Students will do much hands-on problem solving using spreadsheets throughout the course, focusing on analytical model building, logical thinking and managerial insights. Given the nature of the course, this course is a combination of instructor-led lecture sessions, in-class problem-solving exercises, mini-case discussions, and term project presentations. There will be one in-class midterm exam and one final project. The purpose of the final project is to provide students with opportunities to enhance their understanding of the modeling process, and their ability to apply the modeling concepts and the analytical methods covered in this course to a practical business scenario. Students are expected to have some familiarity with MS Excel spreadsheets such as typing, basic arithmetic operations and basic formulas. Strong mathematical background is not required.

### MGT 451R AI & Disruptive Tech Strategy

COURSE DESCRIPTION

How will AI redefine businesses and enable industry transformation in the digital age?

To address this question, we will study the relevant frameworks and tools for formulating AI (Artificial Intelligence) innovation, disruptive technologies, and digital transformation.  It aims to help students gain insights into technology change and business strategy by providing a future-focused lens to how competitive advantage is achieved through innovation, so as to better prepare students to become leaders in the tech-intensive world. Specifically, this course concentrates on understanding the fundamental topics of innovation in both established firms and startups as driven by AI and related technologies. More specifically, we will:

begin with the S curve, a simple but powerful tool to chart disruptive technologies for leadership, startups, investment, and careers;

exam how disruptive technologies (AI in particular) create opportunities for new businesses and transform established ones;

introduce the Technology-Organization-Ecosystem (TOE) theory, distilled from original research;

learn how to make decisions about investment in technologies and new ventures;

zoom on emerging technologies (e.g., Generative AI, LLM, embodied AI, robotics, FSD, data analytics, Fin Tech, block chain, digital currency…);

go through key topics such as AI+X, digital transformation, platform leadership, tech standards, user-centric innovation, technology finance, real options, social media & marketing, and digital healthcare.

look into the movers, shakers, and game changers along the new S curves in many industries redefined by AI and disruptive technologies.

OBJECTIVES

Learn to think like a technology leader with vision and foresight;

Get inside disruptive technologies (AI in particular) and how they will offer the best opportunities (along with the challenges) for business leaders;

Learn the TOE theory and S curves to discriminate between trends and fads;

Learn to make better decisions on technologies, products, markets, and investments;

Identify and evaluate the transformative impact of AI technologies across industries such as hi-tech, finance, healthcare, and education, focusing on their potential to drive innovation and productivity;

Assess the social, ethical and legal implications of AI, developing the skills to navigate challenges related to governance, security and accountability;

Propose innovative AI initiatives or startups that address industry pain points or unmet needs, demonstrating their strategic value and feasibility;

Get positioned into great career paths of a venture capitalist, technology leader, entrepreneur, consultant, investment banker, technology analyst, product manager, financial analyst, or whatever career you’d like to pursue as long as innovation is part of the skill set.

### MGTA 459 Managerial Judgment and Decision Making

This course is composed of two units.  During our initial five class sessions, we will study human judgment and decision making.  We will be particularly concerned with how people perceive and react to uncertainty.  Consider a biotech start-up that is attempting to develop a new cancer drug.  Suppose the firm’s managers must choose between divergent research approaches, each of which shows some promise but also carries some risks.  How will the managers weigh the promise versus the risks?  How will they evaluate relevant information, and how will they decide what further information to pursue?  The hope is that a savvy understanding of the answers to such questions can inform your analysis in many business settings.

Of course, it is not enough to be skillful at analysis.  To succeed in business, you must also be adept at engaging others and getting their buy-in for the insights you draw from your analysis.  For instance, the managers of the biotech start-up may wish to motivate employees and other stakeholders to devote their energies to a particular research approach.  They may wish to rally investors around funding this approach.  Accordingly, in the latter five class sessions, we will study how to best exert influence to achieve your goals.  A key idea is that each of us must develop multiple tools of influence, because different circumstances require different approaches.

### MGTF 401 Financial Accounting

In this course, we will examine fundamental accounting concepts and principles. The course is intended to provide you with the basic skills necessary to understand and analyze financial statements as well as to prepare you for more advanced financial statement analysis courses. The objective is not to train you as an accountant but rather to help you become an informed user of financial statements. ### MGTF 402 Investment Analysis

Building on the idea of a risk-return trade-off, this course develops a framework for understanding how investors choose between risky and risk-free financial securities and determine which combinations of assets are desirable. This is the asset allocation or portfolio decision. Finance is inherently a practitioner’s science and we emphasize the practical implementation of asset allocation methods: How do we go from the theory to compute actual numbers using a combination of data and subjective judgment? The aim is to provide experience in applying techniques in common use in the portfolio management industry.

A better understanding of portfolio choice requires knowledge of which components of returns can be diversified and which cannot. It also requires understanding how risk premiums are determined, how well they can be estimated and how stable they are through time. To this end we turn to asset pricing models, including the Capital Asset Pricing Model (CAPM) and develop factor pricing models that characterize risk in terms of securities’ exposures to a range of common sources of risk, including macroeconomic risk factors. This also leads to the idea of style investing – accounting for value, growth and size effects – which is covered in some detail.

Is money left on the table and are these models any good in practice? To discuss this, we turn to market efficiency and behavioral finance. We will introduce tools to test for market efficiency using so-called event study methodology. If anyone can outperform, presumably it is professional fund managers. We therefore study mutual funds and hedge funds and discuss how to evaluate their performance on a risk-adjusted basis using the asset pricing models developed in the previous part of the course.

The course next turns to bond pricing, including how to understand the relationship between interest rates at different maturities (the term structure of interest rates), how to measure the risk of a single bond and how to manage the risk of a bond portfolio. How information can be extracted from the term structure of interest rates will also be discussed.

The final topic covered in the course is derivative securities such as options, forwards and futures. We characterize their payoffs, look at their pricing, including binomial lattice methods and the Black-Scholes option pricing formula and discuss how they can be used in investment strategies.

The course engages participants in the empirical analysis of a variety of financial data sets. While most of the assignments can be done in Excel, knowledge of programming languages such as Python is beneficial.

### MGTF 404 Financial Econometrics and Empirical Methods

This course is designed to help students bridge the gap between theoretical financial models and the world of financial econometrics and empirics. Have a good understanding of the important topics in empirical finance.  Be able to implement many of the widely-used methods in modern empirical finance.  Have in-depth knowledge in an area of their choice.

### MGTF 405 Business Forecasting

Forecasts of uncertain future outcomes or events form a key input into most business and investment decisions and so affect all areas of finance and business practice. Stock pickers and investment analysts need to forecast future returns on the stocks they consider investing in. Airlines need to forecast fuel prices in order to determine whether to hedge their exposure to future oil prices. Companies need to forecast both the size and variability of their future cash flows to determine whether to use debt or equity in their financing decisions. The Federal Reserve decides current interest rates based on their predictions of future inflation and economic activity. Home builders need to plan their construction activities years in advance and so have to predict both house prices and demand for homes before committing millions of dollars into essentially irreversible investment projects. Pharmaceutical and IT firms are faced with both technological uncertainty (which products are feasible?) and uncertainties about future market size, competitors’ behavior and market shares. Predictions of both will affect which projects should be launched immediately, which ones should be canceled and which ones should be postponed.  This course introduces students to quantitative methods for producing their own state-of-the-art forecasts based on information from sources such as past values of the variable(s) being predicted, surveys, market information and other relevant economic data. It also teaches course participants to become critical consumers of forecasts reported in the media and by professional forecasters. Are such forecasts of any value and, if so, how good is a forecaster’s track record?  Although emphasis will be put on integrating forecasts with the underlying “economic story” and the forecaster’s objective function, the course relies extensively on quantitative methods. Empirical methods such as regression analysis, estimation and graphical tools lie at the core of the course and will be used extensively.

### MGTF 415 Collecting and Analyzing Financial Data

The skill of obtaining and analyzing data to solve business problems is invaluable in the financial services. In this course, students will learn how to scrutinize data, paying attention to the sources, time stamps, unique identifiers, and many other subtle features. Students will solve applied problems using data sources from across different sub-sectors of finance, including asset management, banking, real estate, and private equity. ### MGTF 418 Preparing for the CFA Exam

The Chartered Financial Analyst (CFA) designation is the industry-accepted gold standard for financial analysts. To earn the CFA charter you must pass three very challenging exams. Exam material draws from surveys of practicing analysts and covers 10 major topics. We will look at each of the 10 major topics areas (one per class) and explore the level of understanding that is required to pass the first level exam. We will do applied valuation work as tested on the actual CFA exams. We will highlight study habits of successful past candidates. We will pay special attention to sub-topics that aren’t part of the core RADY MFIN program but that are covered on the CFA exams.  Class-size permitting, candidates will get the opportunity to examine tested CFA financial metrics on the 2nd floor Bloomberg terminals.
